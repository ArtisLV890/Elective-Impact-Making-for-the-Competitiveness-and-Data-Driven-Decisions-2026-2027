We chose these four because each rests on an **existing rule, institution or funding line**, so none needs new legislation to start. Each answers a weakness that the Peru evidence or the Latvian data exposes.

# Idea 1. Make Latvijas Banka's teacher seminars count in full

## The problem

- Latvijas Banka runs a teacher seminar cycle, **"Finanšu pratība praksē"**: **12 personal-finance topics over 3 years**, in person and online.
- Each seminar is a half day. The first ran 10:00–13:30, about 3.5 hours.
- Teachers need **36 hours of training every 3 years** (Reg. 569, point 15).
- **If the seminars are not an approved programme, they can only be counted under point 19.** The school head may credit conferences and seminars, but **at most 12 hours in 3 years**, and that cap is **shared with every other conference a teacher attends**.
- **The result:** a teacher who attends all 12 seminars (about 40 hours) may get **12 hours or fewer** credited.
- So the seminars compete with courses that count in full. This matches our expectation that they "don't count because of time". Whether Latvijas Banka issues a certificate today is **[verify with Latvijas Banka]**.

## The legal route

Regulation No. 569, point 21, lists who may run a programme that counts in full:

- **21.2:** institutions subordinate to ministries, or municipal institutions whose bylaws include education, **or universities that run teacher-training programmes**. No extra approval is named.
- **21.1:** municipal or private schools and **NGOs** whose statutes include teacher training, with **approval from the municipality** where the course runs.

**Latvijas Banka is independent of the ministries**, so on our reading it is not itself a 21.2 provider. The fix is to **deliver the same content through a provider that is.**

## The design

1. **Provider.**
   - **VIAA** (subordinate to the Ministry of Education; it took over VISC's functions in 2025; it runs the ESF+ teacher-support project 4.2.2.3) **or a university with teacher education**, such as LU, LiepU or DU.
   - Latvijas Banka supplies the content and the trainers.
2. **Write it up as a programme** (point 22 lists what is needed): aim, tasks, learning outcomes, format, target group, an hours-based plan, topics and methods. The provider issues the certificate (*apliecība*, Annex 1).
3. **Package it into modules.** Three seminars plus a short task make a module of about **12 hours**. Four modules cover the full cycle and can make up a teacher's **entire 36-hour obligation**.
4. **Make it a proper course, not a lecture series:**
   - **A short knowledge check** at the start and the end. This also gives Latvijas Banka the teacher data it found lacking in 2024.
   - **A classroom task between seminars:** teach one lesson and report back. Peru found that teachers who actually taught the material gained **+0.46 SD**, against +0.32 SD for all trained teachers.
5. **Keep the online option,** so rural teachers can join.

## Who gains

- **Latvijas Banka:** more teachers attend, and the 2021–2027 strategy gets a measurable teacher result before it ends.
- **VIAA:** ready content for its teacher-support system, at no development cost.
- **Teachers:** the hours count in full, and the content is useful for their own finances too.
- **Our proposal:** it is the quick win that works **before** any regulation changes. It is also the delivery channel for the later required 12 hours (Idea C in the legal note).

- **[verify]** that VIAA's bylaws include education activities, as point 21.2 requires.

# Idea 2. Teacher placements in the finance sector

## The problem

- Fraud techniques change faster than any textbook.
- Teachers' knowledge of interest is weak (Latvijas Banka 2024).
- Teenagers are being recruited as money mules on Telegram.
- Teachers need **current, concrete cases**. These sit inside banks' fraud and anti-money-laundering units.

## The legal hook

Regulation No. 569, **point 19**, lets the school head credit, **up to 12 hours in 3 years**, activities including **"stažēšanās nozares uzņēmumos"** (placements in sector companies) and **"dalība darba devēju organizāciju rīkotajās mācībās"** (training run by employers' organisations).

- **Placements in banks therefore count today**, with no new rule.
- They fall inside the same 12-hour cap as conferences. So this works best **alongside** Idea 1, which makes the seminars count in full and frees the 12-hour space.

## The design: "A day at the fraud desk"

- **Format:** one placement day (about 6 hours), or two days for the full 12 hours, at:
  - a bank's fraud-prevention or anti-money-laundering unit;
  - Latvijas Banka;
  - a payment or fintech company;
  - optionally a mobile operator, since phone fraud caused the largest losses in 2025 (€13.2m).
- **Content:**
  1. what real fraud attempts look like;
  2. how banks spot money-mule accounts;
  3. what happens to a young person whose account is used;
  4. how to report;
  5. how complaints and refunds work.
- **Output:** each teacher leaves with **one ready classroom case** based on real, anonymised scenarios.
- **Host group:** Finance Latvia Association members, the four largest banks. They already prevented **€14m** of fraud in 2025 and ran about 18,000 interventions, so they have the material.
- **Scale (illustration):** 4 banks × 4 placement days a year × 15 teachers is about **240 teachers a year**, which would reach most social-studies teachers within 3 years. The teacher count is **[verify with IZM]**.

## Why banks would do it

- **Liability is moving to banks.** Under the EU Payment Services Regulation (political deal November 2025; formal adoption and application date **[verify]**), a bank must refund customers tricked by someone **impersonating the bank** (Article 59), provided the customer reports it.
  - Every scam prevented is money saved.
  - Teaching the next generation is cheap prevention.
- **They are already active.** Finance Latvia runs the "Neuzķeries" campaign and sent 17 fraud proposals to the Interior Ministry in 2026, **none on schools**. This gives them a school strand.
- **Low cost:** staff time, a meeting room and a fixed programme.

## Safeguards (essential for schools and ministries)

- **Follow the OECD/INFE Guidelines for Private and Not-for-profit Stakeholders in Financial Education:**
  - keep education separate from marketing;
  - make content balanced and impartial;
  - disclose and manage conflicts of interest.
- **The OECD is consulting on an updated version right now** (15 September – 2 October 2026), so Latvia can adopt the new text.
- **A simple written agreement:**
  - no products or logos in classroom materials;
  - no data about pupils;
  - case material reviewed by Latvijas Banka.

## Steps

| When | Step |
|---|---|
| Feb 2027 | Pitch to the Finance Latvia board, framed as fraud prevention |
| Apr 2027 | Pilot: two placement days with one bank and one municipality's teachers (for example Riga, whose education board plans teacher training) |
| Sept 2027 | Rollout across banks; add to Latvijas Banka's Financial Literacy Working Group plan |

# Idea 3. An unbranded industry fund, using tax relief

## The problem

- **Programmes stop when money stops.** In Peru, training and monitoring ended after one year; the region that re-ran it got the biggest effects.
- Latvian public budgets are tight, and EU funds for teacher support (ESF+ 4.2.2.3, about €49.7m) run to 2026.
- **But branded corporate money in schools is a red line** for ministries and parents.

## The mechanism

- **Donations to a public-benefit organisation** (*sabiedriskā labuma organizācija*, SLO) give companies **corporate income tax relief**.
- A company can reduce the tax due on distributed profit by **85% of the donated amount, up to 30% of the tax due** (State Revenue Service ruling, 2025). Check the current wording of the Corporate Income Tax Law before quoting figures **[verify]**.
- **Since 2026, companies can also donate services**, with a written donation agreement (amendments adopted by the Saeima on 21 May 2026). Fraud analysts' time, printing or design work could be donated, not just money.

## The design: a pooled fund

- **Donors:** banks, insurers, payment firms, fintechs and mobile operators. Pool their money so **no single brand** is attached to any lesson.
- **Holder:**
  - an existing public-benefit organisation or foundation;
  - or Jaunie Ekonomisti, if it has, or obtains, public-benefit status **[verify]**.
- **Governance:**
  - content is decided by Latvijas Banka and VIAA, **not by donors**;
  - an annual public report;
  - donors are listed only in that report.
- **What it pays for:**
  1. delivery costs of the approved teacher course (Idea 1);
  2. printing and updating materials;
  3. independent evaluation (before and after knowledge tests);
  4. multi-year continuity, through a **3-year pledge**.
- **Size:** our estimate for teacher training is **€150–250k once**, plus modest annual costs. A handful of donors can cover this.

## Why donors would join

- **Tax relief** lowers the real cost.
- **Fraud prevention** fits the new liability regime (Idea 2).
- **It is visible commitment** under the EU Financial Literacy Strategy and the OECD plan for Latvia, **without the risk of being seen to advertise to children**.

## Risks

- **Donor fatigue.** Ask for 3-year pledges, not one-off gifts.
- **Perceived influence.** Strict governance and an annual public report.
- **Tax details change.** Have a tax adviser confirm before launch.

# Idea 4. Media: three channels with real hooks

The goal is not "more press releases". It is **sustained attention that keeps the policy window open**, plus **reaching teenagers where they actually are**.

## 4a. Public media (LSM): the 2027 public-service order

**The hook.** In February 2026 the public-media regulator **SEPLP** reported that audiences rate LSM's economics coverage worse than before:

- the share of LSM users who say it gives new, useful knowledge about business fell from **48% to 43%**;
- Latvian Television fell from **48% to 42%**, and Latvian Radio from **48% to 41%**.

Other findings from the same survey:

- **30%** of people name the economy as a top-3 problem.
- **Minority-language audiences are more interested in economics.**
- People who rarely use public media turn to **Telegram, WhatsApp and bloggers** instead.

SEPLP concluded that LSM should **strengthen economics content**. It pointed to **dedicated thematic teams with regular content** as the model that works.

**The window.** The **2027 public-service order** (*sabiedriskais pasūtījums*) is being prepared now, alongside amendments to the law and LSM's strategy.

**The ask.** A recurring **personal-finance and fraud strand** in the 2027 order:

- a short weekly format;
- a youth-facing version;
- Russian-language reach on LSM's new unified portal.

**How:**

- submit a proposal to SEPLP and LSM during the 2027 order process, using SEPLP's own numbers;
- offer the expert pool (Latvijas Banka, State Police, Finance Latvia) and student voices.

**[verify]** the consultation steps and deadlines for the 2027 order.

## 4b. Commercial and regional media: the Media Support Fund

**The hook.** The state **Media Support Fund** (*Mediju atbalsta fonds*), run by the Society Integration Fund (SIF), finances media content of public importance:

- in 2026, **37 national projects** (macro and micro calls) and **62 regional, local, diaspora and cooperation projects** (about **€1.9m**).

**The idea.**

- **We do not apply for this money.** Media outlets do.
- We bring editors a **ready series concept**, for example:
  - "Who recruits our kids as money mules";
  - "A day at the bank's fraud desk", linked to Idea 2;
  - "Why Latvia is last in the EU on financial literacy".
- We offer data, experts and access. The outlet applies to the Fund.
- **Regional media matter:** LSM reports that people in Latgale meet phone scammers more often than elsewhere in Latvia, with €1.34m stolen there in ten months.

**[verify]** the 2027 call themes and dates on sif.gov.lv.

## 4c. Social media and finfluencers

**The problem.** Teenagers learn about money from the internet, not school:

- 46% of 9th graders often get money information online;
- only 9% get it at school;
- under-35s prefer social media for fraud information (OECD 2026).

**The hooks:**

- **ESMA's January 2026 finfluencer factsheet in Latvian:**
  - disclose paid promotions clearly;
  - no personal investment advice without a licence.
- **PTAC enforcement:**
  - its 2024 checks found unlabelled ads;
  - in 2026 it warned about unlabelled commercial and AI-generated content and **blocked social-media accounts promoting instant loans**.

**The idea: a "responsible creator" pledge.**

- Creators who follow the ESMA and PTAC rules sign a short pledge.
- In return they get **fact-checking and expert access from Latvijas Banka** and a simple badge.
- Pledged creators get first access to new data and campaigns, such as Financial Literacy Week in March.
- This turns the channel that harms young people into one that helps. Enforcement stays with PTAC and Latvijas Banka.

## 4d. A predictable news calendar

| When | Event | Data |
|---|---|---|
| March | Financial Literacy Week | Annual progress report on our KPIs |
| Every 2 years | Latvijas Banka 9th-grade survey | Headline: is the "never learn at school" share falling? |
| November | OECD / Latvijas Banka fraud data | Fraud figures with a youth angle |
| When regulations move | TAP consultation, Cabinet decision | "Compulsory on paper → in practice" milestone |

**Rule:** use credible data only (Latvijas Banka, OECD, Eurobarometer). Our own survey is illustration, never a headline.

# How the four ideas fit together

1. **Idea 1** makes training worth teachers' time and is the delivery channel.
2. **Idea 2** gives that training live, current content, and gives banks a role that is not advertising.
3. **Idea 3** pays for the next years, so the programme does not stop like Peru's did in 2017.
4. **Idea 4** keeps public and political attention on the issue while regulations are written (2027), and reaches teenagers directly.

They feed the regulation track: approved courses (Idea 1) are what the proposed required 12 hours (new point 20.2 of Reg. 569) would run on.
