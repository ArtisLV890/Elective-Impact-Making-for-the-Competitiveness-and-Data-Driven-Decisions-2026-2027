**How they fit:**

- Idea 3 makes sure the topic is taught **within the existing standard**, through VIAA's programmes, materials and tests.
- Ideas 1 and 2 make sure teachers **can and do** teach it. Idea 1 is the voluntary quick win; Idea 2 makes it stick.
- Idea 4 **proves** whether it worked, and keeps the issue in public view.

All four use existing institutions and rules. None needs a new law or new money from companies.

# Idea 1. Training that counts

## The problem

- **Latvijas Banka's teacher seminar cycle,** *"Finanšu pratība praksē"*, launched in 2026: 12 personal-finance topics over 3 years, half-day sessions (the first ran 10:00–13:30), in person and on MS Teams.
- **Teachers must complete 36 hours of training every 3 years** (Reg. 569, point 15).
- **A seminar that is not part of an approved programme counts only under point 19.** The school head may credit conferences and seminars, but **at most 12 hours in 3 years**, and that cap is **shared with every other conference**.
- **So a teacher who attends the whole cycle (about 40 hours) may get 12 hours or fewer credited.** Teachers then pick courses that count in full.
- Latvijas Banka cannot fix this alone. It is independent of the ministries, so it is **not among the providers point 21 lists** (our reading).

## The legal route (no rule change needed)

Regulation No. 569, point 21.2, lets **institutions subordinate to a ministry whose bylaws include education, and universities that run teacher-training programmes**, run approved programmes.

- **VIAA** is subordinate to the Ministry of Education. It took over VISC's functions in 2025 and runs the teacher-support project 4.2.2.3, so it fits **[verify its bylaws]**.
- **A university with teacher education** fits directly, for example LU, LiepU or DU.
- **Fallback (point 21.1):** an NGO or a municipal body, with the municipality's approval.

The provider issues the certificate of completion (*apliecība*, Annex 1). The programme must state (point 22) its aim, tasks, outcomes, format, target group, an hours-based plan, topics and methods.

## The legal context in full

**1. The chain of rules**

- **Education Law, Art. 14, point 32:** the Cabinet sets the rules for teachers' professional development. This is **Regulation No. 569**.
- **Education Law, Art. 49(3):** development happens through self-study and courses run by institutions that offer continuing education.
- **Education Law, Art. 51(1) point 4:** improving one's competence is a teacher's duty.
- **Education Law, Art. 52(1) point 4:** teachers have the right to 30 paid days every 3 years for it.
- **Education Law, Art. 52(1) point 4.1:** teachers have the right to support from the school head and the school's owner.

**2. What counts toward the 36 hours (Reg. 569)**

| Route | What it is | How much counts | Who decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Approved programme** (point 21) | A course run by an authorised provider, with a certificate (point 23, Annex 1) | **All hours** | The provider issues the certificate; the school head accepts it |
| **Other activities** (point 19) | Conferences, seminars, master classes, placements in companies, employers' training, personal development | **At most 12 hours in 3 years, in total** | The school head |
| **Foreign programmes** (point 19.1) | Foreign institutions' programmes, Erasmus+ | All hours | The school head |

**3. Who may run an approved programme (point 21)**

| Point | Who | Extra approval? |
|---|---|---|
| 21.1 | Municipal or private schools; NGOs whose statutes include teacher training | **Yes:** the municipality where the course is run must approve it. For online courses across many municipalities, this is unclear in practice |
| 21.2 | **Institutions subordinate to a ministry**, or municipal institutions, **whose bylaws include educational activity**; **universities that run teacher-training programmes** | **No** |
| 21.3–21.4 | Universities: 72-hour and 160-hour certificate programmes | Agreed with IZM (not needed here) |
| 21.5 | Foreign institutions in Latvia | Agreed with IZM |

**4. Why Latvijas Banka cannot do it alone**

- Latvijas Banka is an **independent** institution under its own law. It is **not subordinate to any ministry**, and it is not a school, an NGO or a university.
- It therefore does not fit any category in point 21, on our reading.
- Its seminars are, legally, "seminars" under point 19: they count only within the shared **12-hour** cap, and only if the school head accepts them.

**5. Why VIAA fits point 21.2**

- **VIAA's bylaws** (Cabinet Regulation No. 207, 01.04.2025) make it a direct administration body **subordinate to the Minister of Education and Science**.
- Its tasks include:
  - **professional and methodological support for teachers, school leaders and municipal education specialists** (point 4.13);
  - **methodological materials and teaching aids** (4.14);
  - **the curriculum standards and sample subject programmes** (4.5);
  - **the content of state tests** (4.16).
- Its predecessor VISC issued teacher-training certificates itself. Regulation No. 569, point 32, refers to "certificates issued by VISC" for the swimming course.
- So VIAA can run the course **without anyone else's approval**, and its certificate counts in full.
- A university with teacher-training programmes is the equivalent alternative.

**6. What the partnership needs in practice**

1. **A cooperation agreement** between Latvijas Banka and VIAA (or the university): Latvijas Banka provides content and trainers; the provider owns the programme.
2. **A programme description** meeting point 22: aim, tasks, outcomes, format, target group, an hours-based plan, topics and methods.
3. **Certificates** issued by the provider on the Annex 1 form, one per module.
4. **Nothing changes** in Regulation No. 569, and no Cabinet decision is needed.

**7. Still to confirm**

- Whether any Latvijas Banka seminars already count through a provider.
- VIAA's own procedure for adopting external content into its teacher-support offer.


## The design

**Four modules of about 12 hours.** Each module is three half-day seminars plus a short classroom task. One module fills a third of the 36 hours; all four cover them entirely.

| Module | Content (aimed at the known gaps) | Classroom task |
|---|---|---|
| A. Money, interest and inflation | Budgeting; **simple and compound interest** (teachers' known weak spot, Latvijas Banka 2024); inflation | Teach one lesson on compound interest |
| B. Credit and debt | Consumer credit, instant loans, "buy now, pay later", the real cost of borrowing | Pupils compare two loan offers |
| C. Fraud and digital safety | Phishing, purchase scams, investment fraud, **money mules**, how and where to report | Pupils analyse a real (anonymised) scam message |
| D. Saving, investing, pensions, insurance | Risk and return, the pension pillars, insurance basics | Pupils plan a first salary |

*Latvijas Banka's 12 seminar topics would be grouped into these modules; its exact topic list is still to be checked.*

**Three features that make it more than a lecture series:**

1. **A short knowledge check** before and after each module. This gives Latvijas Banka the teacher data it lacked.
2. **The classroom task between seminars.** In Peru, teachers who actually taught the material gained **+0.46 SD** in their own knowledge, against +0.32 SD for all trained teachers.
3. **An online option,** so teachers outside Riga can join.

## Who gains

- **Latvijas Banka:** more teachers attend, and its 2021–2027 strategy shows a teacher result before it ends.
- **VIAA:** ready content for its teacher-support system, with no development cost.
- **Teachers:** the hours count in full, and the content helps their own finances.
- **Our case:** a working route exists before any regulation changes. Idea 2 then makes it compulsory.

2. Draft the programme description to point 22's checklist, so the partners only have to edit.
3. Track take-up and report it in the yearly scorecard (Idea 4).

# Idea 2. Required, targeted training

## The problem

- Today teachers choose their own training freely.
- The only topic required of **every** teacher is child-rights protection, at least 6 hours (Reg. 569, point 20).
- Peru shows what voluntary training produces: **43%** of teachers attended all the training, and **a third** never taught the lessons.

## The precedent

- **Since 1 September 2025, PE teachers who teach swimming must complete 24 hours of training** in swimming instruction and water safety (Reg. 569, point 20.1; Regulation No. 354 of 10.06.2025).
- **The government's reasoning** (annotation to draft 24-TA-3026):
  - not all PE teachers who teach swimming had the knowledge and practice needed;
  - the aim was pupils' safety and fewer drowning accidents.
- **Our case has the same structure:**
  - **a knowledge gap:** Latvijas Banka found teachers' grasp of simple and compound interest insufficient;
  - **a risk to pupils:**
    - 48% of 9th graders were offered "easy money" online;
    - 18% were asked to be money mules;
    - fraud losses rose from €8.5m (2023) to €23.7m (2025).

## Draft wording (illustrative, to be refined with lawyers)

> **20.2** Vispārējās izglītības pedagogs, kas māca mācību priekšmetu "Sociālās zinības" pamatizglītības 7.–9. klasē vai mācību kursu "Sociālās zinātnes I", "Sociālās zinātnes II" vai "Sociālās zinības un vēsture" vispārējās vidējās izglītības pakāpē, triju gadu laikā pilnveido profesionālo kompetenci finanšu pratībā, tai skaitā finanšu drošībā digitālajā vidē, vismaz 12 stundu apjomā šo noteikumu 21. punktā norādītajās programmās.

*In English:* a general-education teacher who teaches "Social studies" in grades 7–9, or "Social sciences I", "Social sciences II" or "Social studies and history" at secondary level, completes at least 12 hours of financial literacy training, including financial safety online, every 3 years, in approved programmes.

**Transitional provision (draft):**

> Šo noteikumu 20.2 punktā noteikto prasību piemēro ar 2028. gada 1. septembri; pedagogs to izpilda līdz 2031. gada 31. augustam.

*In English:* applies from 1 September 2028; teachers must comply by 31 August 2031.

## Why this design is easy to accept

1. **No extra workload.** The 12 hours sit **inside** the 36 teachers already owe.
2. **Narrow scope.** Only teachers who teach the topic are covered. That is a few hundred to about a thousand people **[verify with IZM]**, not every teacher in Latvia.
3. **Courses exist first.** Idea 1 provides approved courses before the requirement starts, as was done for the swimming rule.
4. **It follows a model the ministry wrote itself a year ago.**

## The process

1. **IZM drafts** the amendment with an annotation explaining the problem, the solution, the impact and the consultation.
2. **Public consultation on the TAP portal.** Anyone can submit an opinion.
3. **Announcement at the State Secretaries' meeting;** ministries give their opinions.
4. **Cabinet adopts** the amendment.

**How we push it:**

- send IZM the draft wording and a two-page annotation-style case;
- ask the Saeima financial literacy subcommittee to hold a hearing and back it;
- ask Latvijas Banka's working group (28 partners, including IZM) to include it in its plan;
- cite the OECD action plan for Latvia (November 2026), which calls financial literacy in schools "essential" (cross-cutting Action 4).

## Likely objections and answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Teachers are overloaded." | It is inside the existing 36 hours: no extra hours or days |
| "Why this topic and not others?" | The same logic as swimming: a measurable risk to pupils and a documented knowledge gap |
| "Are there enough courses?" | Yes, Latvijas Banka's cycle via VIAA or a university (Idea 1), online included |
| "Who pays?" | Delivery runs on existing structures (Latvijas Banka content, VIAA's teacher-support system); the cost is mainly teachers' time, which is already budgeted |

## Measures of success

- The amendment is adopted by 2028.
- **80% of covered teachers compliant by 31 August 2031.**

# Idea 3. Teach it within the current standard

## Why changing the standard is hard

1. **It is a Cabinet regulation** (Reg. 747 for basic school, Reg. 416 for secondary).
   - Every change goes through IZM drafting, public consultation and the Cabinet.
   - It is usually bundled into bigger reforms. Reg. 747 was last amended in April and September 2026 (Nos. 221 and 540), so the next opening is uncertain.
2. **The standard is deliberately general and competence-based** (Skola2030).
   - Each subject group defends its outcomes.
   - Adding outcomes means arguing that something else is less important.
3. **Time is fixed in the same regulation.** Social studies in grades 7–9 has **105 hours over three years** (Annex 11), about one lesson a week. More finance time means changing the study plan, which is the hardest part of all.
4. **Minimum lessons cannot be imposed through the sample programmes.**
   - The subject sample programmes that VIAA maintains are **recommendations**.
   - Under the Education Law (Art. 52(1) point 5.1), **teachers decide how to deliver the subject content and choose their methods**, within the standard and the school's programme.
   - So "minimum 12 lessons" is not enforceable without changing the standard or the study plan.

**Conclusion:** asking for new outcomes and minimum hours is a long shot. We keep it only as a fallback (below). The main route works **inside the existing standard**.

## What the current standard already allows

**By the end of grade 9, the existing outcomes already cover most of what we need:**

| Topic we want taught | Existing outcome it fits (Reg. 747) |
|---|---|
| Cost of credit, "buy now, pay later", saving vs borrowing | **Social area 5.4:** "makes responsible decisions by evaluating the benefits of services offered by various financial institutions … the possibility of deposits and borrowing" |
| Phishing, sharing personal data, account takeover | **Social area 3.3:** "observes the rules on … personal data protection and evaluates the consequences of not observing them" |
| Inflation, the central bank, taxes | **Social area 5.2** |
| Budgeting and choices | **Social area 5.1** |
| Compound interest, the total cost of a loan | **Maths 3.2.4–3.2.6:** percentage increase and decrease "in situations with a real context", "analyses the use of percentages in daily life and the media", "plans expenses" |
| Money mules | Social 3.3 (consequences of misusing one's data and account) together with 5.4. Weakest fit: needs good materials |

**So fraud and compound interest can be taught as examples within outcomes that already exist.** What is missing is not legal permission. It is **guidance, materials and a reason for teachers to prioritise it**.

## The design: three VIAA levers, no regulation change

All three sit within **VIAA's own functions** under its bylaws (Cabinet Regulation No. 207, 2025):

- **sample programmes** (point 4.5);
- **methodological materials and teaching aids** (point 4.14);
- **the content of state tests** (point 4.16).

**1. A finance thread in the sample subject programmes** (social studies 7–9, maths 8–9)

- Mark where each existing outcome is taught with a financial example, and suggest a sequence:
  - grade 7: budget and choices;
  - grade 8: saving, credit and the cost of borrowing;
  - grade 9: fraud, data and money mules.
- **Recommended, not compulsory,** but this is the document teachers plan from.

**2. Ready-made materials tagged to outcome numbers**

- Lesson plans, worksheets and real (anonymised) scam examples, built from Latvijas Banka's Naudas skola material and published by VIAA.
- Each material names the outcome it serves, for example "Social 5.4" or "Maths 3.2.4", so teachers see it is part of the required content, not an extra.
- Latvijas Banka already plans to add to teaching materials (LSM, February 2026).

**3. Financial-context items in the state tests VIAA designs**

- **Examples:** a compound-interest or loan-cost task in maths; a scam-recognition task in social studies.
- **Why it works:** these are **within the existing outcomes**, so no regulation is needed, and **what is tested gets taught**.
- **[verify]** which 9th-grade tests run in 2027 and later (exams, monitoring tests, diagnostic tests) and their cycle.

**Why this works with teacher autonomy, not against it:**

- It does not force minutes in class.
- It changes **what teachers find in their planning documents, what materials they get, and what their pupils are tested on**.
- Together with Ideas 1 and 2 (trained teachers), that is how Peru's lesson becomes practice.

## The fallback: if the redesign opens the standard

If IZM's social-studies redesign does reopen Annex 3, we submit three short additions:

**(a)** A new grade-9 outcome on fraud:

> Atpazīst biežāk sastopamos finanšu krāpšanas veidus (pikšķerēšana, pirkumu krāpšana, ieguldījumu krāpšana) un naudas pārvedēja shēmas, izvērtē to sekas sev un citiem un zina, kā rīkoties un kur ziņot.

*In English:* recognises the most common types of financial fraud (phishing, purchase fraud, investment fraud) and money-mule schemes, assesses their consequences for themselves and others, and knows how to act and where to report.

**(b)** An extension of 5.4:

> … tai skaitā patēriņa kredītu un atliktā maksājuma pakalpojumus, izvērtējot to kopējās izmaksas un risku.

*In English:* … including consumer credit and buy-now-pay-later, assessing their total cost and risk.

**(c)** An extension of maths 3.2.4:

> … tai skaitā secīgas procentuālās izmaiņas (salikti procenti), aprēķinot uzkrājuma pieaugumu un aizņēmuma kopējās izmaksas.

*In English:* … including successive percentage changes (compound interest), calculating the growth of savings and the total cost of a loan.

**These are small:** one new outcome and two extensions of existing ones. That is the easiest form of change to accept. But **we do not depend on them**.

# Idea 4. Measure it and make it visible

## The problem

- Latvia's school financial literacy has been measured only **once**: Latvijas Banka's 9th-grade assessment (February–April 2025, 1,159 pupils, 75 schools, index 12.3/20).
- **No repeat is announced.**
- **Latvia is in PISA**: reading, maths and science, every cycle since 2000. **Financial literacy is a separate, optional PISA module** that each country chooses.
- Latvia took the optional module in **2012 (501 points) and 2018 (501)**, but **skipped it in 2022**. There was no financial literacy module in 2025.
- **Without regular data**, no one can tell whether new rules work, and the topic drops out of the news.

## The design

1. **Latvijas Banka repeats the 9th-grade assessment every 2 years** (2027, 2029, 2031), with the same questions, and adds:
   - a short **teacher module** (knowledge of interest and fraud);
   - the question **"how often do you learn about money at school?"**, which is the delivery check for Idea 3.
2. **Latvia opts into the PISA 2029 financial literacy module again** (a secondary ask; the Latvijas Banka survey is the core).
   - PISA 2029 offers it for the fifth time (38 countries have taken part since 2012).
   - It needs at least 10 participating countries, and its cost is set once the number is known.
   - The decision lies with IZM **[verify the decision deadline and cost]**.
   - Latvia's last result was 501 points in 2018, against an OECD average of 505 and Estonia's 547. Taking part again shows whether the gap to Estonia closes.
3. **A yearly public scorecard in Financial Literacy Week (March).**
   - One page and five indicators, compiled by Jaunie Ekonomisti from credible sources only.
   - **The indicators:**
     - 9th-grade index;
     - "never learn about money at school";
     - teachers certified (Idea 1);
     - regulation milestones (Ideas 2 and 3);
     - fraud losses.
   - Our own survey is never used as a headline.
4. **Optional media strand: LSM's 2027 public-service order.**
   - **The finding:** in February 2026 the public-media regulator (SEPLP) found audiences rate LSM's economics coverage worse (useful knowledge about business: **48% → 43%**). It recommended dedicated thematic teams with regular content.
   - **The timing:** by law (Art. 11(1)) SEPLP approves each year's plan by 31 December, so the 2027 plan is almost final. The realistic target is the **2028 plan**.
   - **The legal limit:** specific programmes cannot be ordered (Art. 9(5)). Ask SEPLP for a task and indicator, not a show. See the legal review.
   - **The ask:** a task and indicator on economic and financial literacy content, for example reach among 15–24-year-olds.
   - This costs us one proposal letter, using SEPLP's own numbers.

## Why it works

- **Measurement is what turned Peru's pilot into evidence.** It is also what the OECD plan for Latvia asks for: regular data, cross-cutting Action 3.
- **A fixed yearly date** gives journalists a reason to return to the topic every March, at no cost to us.
