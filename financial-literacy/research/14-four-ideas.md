| 3 | **A curriculum fix during the redesign.** Add the missing outcomes (fraud, compound interest, money mules) and give finance a minimum place in social studies and maths | Reg. 747 Annex 3 (social area) and maths annex; VIAA sample programme | IZM, VIAA | Draft outcome wording; comment on the course redesign | From 1 Sept 2028 |

**How they fit:**

- Idea 3 decides **what** is taught.
- Ideas 1 and 2 make sure teachers **can and do** teach it. Idea 1 is the voluntary quick win; Idea 2 makes it stick.
- Idea 4 **proves** whether it worked, and keeps the issue in public view.

All four use existing institutions and rules. None needs a new law or new money from companies.

# Idea 1. Training that counts

## The problem

- **Latvijas Banka's teacher seminar cycle,** *"Finanšu pratība praksē"*, launched in 2026: 12 personal-finance topics over 3 years, half-day sessions (the first ran 10:00–13:30), in person and on MS Teams.
- **Teachers must complete 36 hours of training every 3 years** (Reg. 569, point 15).
- **A seminar that is not part of an approved programme counts only under point 19.** The school head may credit conferences and seminars, but **at most 12 hours in 3 years**, and that cap is **shared with every other conference**.
- **So a teacher who attends the whole cycle (about 40 hours) may get 12 hours or fewer credited.** Teachers then pick courses that count in full.
- Latvijas Banka cannot fix this alone. It is independent of the ministries, so it is **not among the providers point 21 lists** (our reading).

## The legal route (no rule change needed)

Regulation No. 569, point 21.2, lets **institutions subordinate to a ministry whose bylaws include education, and universities that run teacher-training programmes**, run approved programmes.

- **VIAA** is subordinate to the Ministry of Education. It took over VISC's functions in 2025 and runs the teacher-support project 4.2.2.3, so it fits **[verify its bylaws]**.
- **A university with teacher education** fits directly, for example LU, LiepU or DU.
- **Fallback (point 21.1):** an NGO or a municipal body, with the municipality's approval.

The provider issues the certificate of completion (*apliecība*, Annex 1). The programme must state (point 22) its aim, tasks, outcomes, format, target group, an hours-based plan, topics and methods.

## The design

**Four modules of about 12 hours.** Each module is three half-day seminars plus a short classroom task. One module fills a third of the 36 hours; all four cover them entirely.

| Module | Content (aimed at the known gaps) | Classroom task |
|---|---|---|
| A. Money, interest and inflation | Budgeting; **simple and compound interest** (teachers' known weak spot, Latvijas Banka 2024); inflation | Teach one lesson on compound interest |
| B. Credit and debt | Consumer credit, instant loans, "buy now, pay later", the real cost of borrowing | Pupils compare two loan offers |
| C. Fraud and digital safety | Phishing, purchase scams, investment fraud, **money mules**, how and where to report | Pupils analyse a real (anonymised) scam message |
| D. Saving, investing, pensions, insurance | Risk and return, the pension pillars, insurance basics | Pupils plan a first salary |

*Latvijas Banka's 12 seminar topics would be grouped into these modules; its exact topic list is still to be checked.*

**Three features that make it more than a lecture series:**

1. **A short knowledge check** before and after each module. This gives Latvijas Banka the teacher data it lacked.
2. **The classroom task between seminars.** In Peru, teachers who actually taught the material gained **+0.46 SD** in their own knowledge, against +0.32 SD for all trained teachers.
3. **An online option,** so teachers outside Riga can join.

## Who gains

- **Latvijas Banka:** more teachers attend, and its 2021–2027 strategy shows a teacher result before it ends.
- **VIAA:** ready content for its teacher-support system, with no development cost.
- **Teachers:** the hours count in full, and the content helps their own finances.
- **Our case:** a working route exists before any regulation changes. Idea 2 then makes it compulsory.

## What Jaunie Ekonomisti does

1. Convene the first meeting: Latvijas Banka, VIAA and one university.
2. Draft the programme description to point 22's checklist, so the partners only have to edit.
3. Track take-up and report it in the yearly scorecard (Idea 4).

## Steps and timeline

|---|---|

| Mar 2027 | Programme description agreed; provider chosen |

## Measures of success

- Number of teachers certified per year.

## Risks

|---|---|

| Latvijas Banka prefers its current format | The seminars stay the same; only the packaging and the certificate change |
| Teachers still do not come | Idea 2 makes it required for the teachers who teach the topic |

# Idea 2. Required, targeted training

## The problem

- Today teachers choose their own training freely.
- The only topic required of **every** teacher is child-rights protection, at least 6 hours (Reg. 569, point 20).
- Peru shows what voluntary training produces: **43%** of teachers attended all the training, and **a third** never taught the lessons.

## The precedent

- **Since 1 September 2025, PE teachers who teach swimming must complete 24 hours of training** in swimming instruction and water safety (Reg. 569, point 20.1; Regulation No. 354 of 10.06.2025).
- **The government's reasoning** (annotation to draft 24-TA-3026):
  - not all PE teachers who teach swimming had the knowledge and practice needed;
  - the aim was pupils' safety and fewer drowning accidents.
- **Our case has the same structure:**
  - **a knowledge gap:** Latvijas Banka found teachers' grasp of simple and compound interest insufficient;
  - **a risk to pupils:**
    - 48% of 9th graders were offered "easy money" online;
    - 18% were asked to be money mules;
    - fraud losses rose from €8.5m (2023) to €23.7m (2025).

## Draft wording (illustrative, to be refined with lawyers)

> **20.2** Vispārējās izglītības pedagogs, kas māca sociālās zinības pamatizglītības 7.–9. klasē vai sociālo zinātņu, ekonomikas vai uzņēmējdarbības mācību kursu vidējās izglītības pakāpē, triju gadu laikā pilnveido profesionālo kompetenci finanšu pratībā, tai skaitā finanšu drošībā digitālajā vidē, vismaz 12 stundu apjomā šo noteikumu 21. punktā norādītajās programmās.

*In English:* a general-education teacher who teaches social studies in grades 7–9, or social sciences, economics or business at secondary level, completes at least 12 hours of financial literacy training, including financial safety online, every 3 years, in approved programmes.

**Transitional provision (draft):**

> Šo noteikumu 20.2 punktā noteikto prasību piemēro ar 2028. gada 1. septembri; pedagogs to izpilda līdz 2031. gada 31. augustam.

*In English:* applies from 1 September 2028; teachers must comply by 31 August 2031.

## Why this design is easy to accept

1. **No extra workload.** The 12 hours sit **inside** the 36 teachers already owe.
2. **Narrow scope.** Only teachers who teach the topic are covered. That is a few hundred to about a thousand people **[verify with IZM]**, not every teacher in Latvia.
3. **Courses exist first.** Idea 1 provides approved courses before the requirement starts, as was done for the swimming rule.
4. **It follows a model the ministry wrote itself a year ago.**

## The process

1. **IZM drafts** the amendment with an annotation explaining the problem, the solution, the impact and the consultation.
2. **Public consultation on the TAP portal.** Anyone can submit an opinion.
3. **Announcement at the State Secretaries' meeting;** ministries give their opinions.
4. **Cabinet adopts** the amendment.

**How we push it:**

- send IZM the draft wording and a two-page annotation-style case;
- ask the Saeima financial literacy subcommittee to hold a hearing and back it;
- ask Latvijas Banka's working group (28 partners, including IZM) to include it in its plan;
- cite the OECD action plan for Latvia (November 2026), which calls financial literacy in schools "essential" (cross-cutting Action 4).

## Likely objections and answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Teachers are overloaded." | It is inside the existing 36 hours: no extra hours or days |
| "Why this topic and not others?" | The same logic as swimming: a measurable risk to pupils and a documented knowledge gap |
| "Are there enough courses?" | Yes, Latvijas Banka's cycle via VIAA or a university (Idea 1), online included |
| "Who pays?" | Delivery runs on existing structures (Latvijas Banka content, VIAA's teacher-support system); the cost is mainly teachers' time, which is already budgeted |

## Measures of success

- The amendment is adopted by 2028.
- **80% of covered teachers compliant by 31 August 2031.**

# Idea 3. Fix the curriculum during the redesign

## The problem

**Time is short.**

- In grades 7–9, social studies gets **105 hours in total over three years**, about one lesson a week (Reg. 747, Annex 11).
- That time is shared with politics, law, ethics and economics.

**The standard already sets some finance outcomes.** By the end of grade 9 (Reg. 747, Annex 3, social and civic area), students should:

- make responsible decisions by comparing financial institutions' services, and understand saving and borrowing (outcome 5.4);
- understand the state budget, taxes, inflation and central banks (5.2);
- understand that resources are limited and choices are needed (5.1).

**But the topics that matter most now are missing:**

- **compound interest and the cost of borrowing;**
- **fraud and digital financial safety;**
- **money mules.**

**Maths stops one step short.** By grade 9 it covers percentages, percentage change and "analysing the use of percentages in daily life and media" (maths annex, 3.2.4–3.2.6), but **not repeated percentage change (compound interest) or comparing loan costs**.

**Latvijas Banka recommends the same fix.** Its 2025 evaluation of the 9th-grade results suggests **structurally integrating finance and economics topics into mathematics**, with teacher preparation and materials.

**The window is open.**

- Reg. 747 was amended twice in 2026 (No. 221 in April; No. 540 in September, applying from 1 September 2026).
- The ministry is considering rebuilding the social-studies course.
- Latvijas Banka plans to add to the teaching materials (LSM, February 2026).

## Draft outcome wording (illustrative)

**Social and civic area, Annex 3, new outcomes by the end of grade 9** (numbering to be fitted to the annex):

> **(a)** Atpazīst biežāk sastopamos finanšu krāpšanas veidus (pikšķerēšana, pirkumu krāpšana, ieguldījumu krāpšana) un naudas pārvedēja shēmas, izvērtē to sekas sev un citiem un zina, kā rīkoties un kur ziņot.

*In English:* recognises the most common types of financial fraud (phishing, purchase fraud, investment fraud) and money-mule schemes, assesses their consequences for themselves and others, and knows how to act and where to report.

> **(b)** Salīdzina uzkrājuma un aizņēmuma iespējas, tai skaitā patēriņa kredītu un atliktā maksājuma pakalpojumus, izvērtē to kopējās izmaksas, risku un ietekmi uz personīgo budžetu.

*In English:* compares saving and borrowing options, including consumer credit and buy-now-pay-later, and assesses their total cost, risk and effect on a personal budget.

**Maths annex, addition to the grade-9 outcomes on percentages:**

> **(c)** … tai skaitā secīgas procentuālās izmaiņas (salikti procenti), aprēķinot uzkrājuma pieaugumu un aizņēmuma kopējās izmaksas.

*In English:* … including successive percentage changes (compound interest), calculating the growth of savings and the total cost of a loan.

## A minimum place in the sample programme

The standard sets outcomes, not hours. Hours are guided by **VIAA's sample subject programmes**. We propose:

| Where | Minimum | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Social studies, grades 7–9 | **About 12 lessons in total** (4 a year), about 11% of the subject's time | Budget and choices; saving and borrowing; fraud and money mules; consumer rights |
| Maths, grades 8–9 | **About 4–6 lessons** using financial contexts | Compound interest, loan cost, comparing offers |
| Secondary (Reg. 416) | Time for the existing detailed outcomes | Budget, investment types, financial markets (banks, shares, bonds, peer-to-peer lending), risks, consumer protection |

**Ready-made lessons:** each block comes with lesson plans and materials (Latvijas Banka's Naudas skola, VIAA). Teachers deliver; they do not invent.

- The outcomes are adopted.
- The sample programme names a finance block.
- The share of 9th graders who say they **never learn about money at school** falls from **36%** to **under 15%** by 2031 (Latvijas Banka survey; see Idea 4).

## Risks

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| "The standard is already full." | Two outcomes, and one clause added to maths. They replace generic examples rather than adding a new subject |
| Teachers lack confidence for maths finance tasks | Ideas 1 and 2 cover maths teachers' needs too (Module A) |
| The redesign takes longer than expected | Outcomes can be added by a separate small amendment |

# Idea 4. Measure it and make it visible

## The problem

- Latvia's school financial literacy has been measured only **once**: Latvijas Banka's 9th-grade assessment (February–April 2025, 1,159 pupils, 75 schools, index 12.3/20).
- **No repeat is announced.**
- Latvia **skipped the PISA 2022 financial literacy test**.
- **Without regular data**, no one can tell whether new rules work, and the topic drops out of the news.

## The design

1. **Latvijas Banka repeats the 9th-grade assessment every 2 years** (2027, 2029, 2031), with the same questions, and adds:
   - a short **teacher module** (knowledge of interest and fraud);
   - the question **"how often do you learn about money at school?"**, which is the delivery check for Idea 3.
2. **Latvia joins the PISA 2029 financial literacy option.**
   - PISA 2029 offers it for the fifth time (38 countries have taken part since 2012).
   - It needs at least 10 participating countries, and its cost is set once the number is known.
   - The decision lies with IZM **[verify the decision deadline and cost]**.
   - Latvia's last result was 501 points in 2018, against an OECD average of 505 and Estonia's 547.
3. **A yearly public scorecard in Financial Literacy Week (March).**
   - One page and five indicators, compiled by Jaunie Ekonomisti from credible sources only.
   - **The indicators:**
     - 9th-grade index;
     - "never learn about money at school";
     - teachers certified (Idea 1);
     - regulation milestones (Ideas 2 and 3);
     - fraud losses.
   - Our own survey is never used as a headline.
4. **Optional media strand: LSM's 2027 public-service order.**
   - **The finding:** in February 2026 the public-media regulator (SEPLP) found audiences rate LSM's economics coverage worse (useful knowledge about business: **48% → 43%**). It recommended dedicated thematic teams with regular content.
   - **The timing:** the **2027 public-service order is being drafted now.**
   - **The ask:** a regular personal-finance and fraud strand, with a youth-facing version.
   - This costs us one proposal letter, using SEPLP's own numbers.

## Why it works

- **Measurement is what turned Peru's pilot into evidence.** It is also what the OECD plan for Latvia asks for: regular data, cross-cutting Action 3.
- **A fixed yearly date** gives journalists a reason to return to the topic every March, at no cost to us.

## What Jaunie Ekonomisti does

1. Propose the biennial survey and teacher module in Latvijas Banka's working group, and for the 2028+ strategy.
2. Ask IZM, in writing, for a decision on PISA 2029 financial literacy.
3. Compile and publish the scorecard every March, starting in 2027.
4. Send the LSM proposal during the 2027 public-service order process.

## Measures of success

- The survey is repeated in 2027.
- IZM confirms PISA 2029 financial literacy.
- The scorecard is published every year and gets media coverage.

## Risks

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Cost of PISA option | Small relative to the €23.7m a year lost to fraud. Ask for it as part of the 2028+ strategy budget |
| Latvijas Banka survey fatigue | Biennial, not annual; same instrument, so results stay comparable |
| Media ignore the scorecard | Tie it to Financial Literacy Week, when outlets already look for finance stories |
