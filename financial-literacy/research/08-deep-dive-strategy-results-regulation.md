> **Update (November 2026).** The strategy has since been narrowed to four ideas: (1) Latvijas Banka's seminars delivered as an approved course, (2) a required 12-hour financial literacy course for social-studies teachers (Regulation No. 569, new point 20.2), (3) teaching within the existing curriculum standard through VIAA's programmes, materials and tests, and (4) measurement (Latvijas Banka survey every 2 years, the PISA 2029 finance module, a yearly scorecard). The gamified practice, family tasks, bank volunteers and school pilot described below are no longer part of the proposal, and the standards are no longer proposed for amendment. See *Our four ideas* and *Legal review - ideas 2 and 4*.


# Summary

> **Key message.** Our strategy works on two tracks at once:
> - **an insider track:** we work inside Latvijas Banka's coalition and with IZM/VIAA;
> - **an outsider track:** media and public pressure, backed by a measured pilot.
>
> Expected results come in three layers:
> 1. **policy changes by 2028** (the part we can most directly influence);
> 2. **learning gains by 2030**, realistically +0.15 to +0.30 SD, with our earlier targets as *stretch* goals;
> 3. **long-term behaviour and fraud effects.**
>
> The regulatory route needs **no new law**: Cabinet regulations, the next **Education Development Guidelines 2028+** (a Cabinet-approved policy document) and a voluntary partnership strategy.

**Three corrections and refinements compared with earlier versions**

1. **The National Financial Literacy Strategy 2021–2027 is a partnership document.** FKTK developed it and Latvijas Banka coordinates it, with 20 partners in 2021 and 28 in 2026. We found no evidence that the Cabinet approved it. The **Cabinet-level anchor** for schools is the **Education Development Guidelines** (2021–2027 approved by Cabinet Order No. 436, June 2021, after support from the Saeima Education Committee). We therefore target **both**.
2. **Minimum lessons belong in the sample programmes** (the annexes to Regulations No. 747 and No. 416, maintained with VIAA). The standards themselves set **learning outcomes**. We propose outcomes in the standard and a minimum block in the sample programme.
3. **The KPI targets are recalibrated.** Using research effect sizes, the realistic *base case* is a 9th-grade index of about **13.2** and about **68%** solving simple interest. The earlier **14.0** and **75%** become *stretch* targets.

# Part A. Strategy deep dive

## A1. From problem to solution: cause → step → evidence

| Cause (Latvian evidence) | Our step | Why it should work (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| Finance is diluted inside social studies; **36%** of 9th graders never learn about money at school | **Module:** 10–12 dedicated lessons (grade 9, repeated at grade 11) | Intensity drives effects (Kaiser & Menkhoff 2017, 2020); dedicated courses work, paper mandates don't (Urban et al. 2020; Cole et al. 2016) |
| Teachers' knowledge of interest is insufficient; only **~9%** of strategy activities target teachers | **Teacher training:** certify Latvijas Banka's seminar cycle and count it in the 36-hour requirement | Teacher training is a key ingredient (Compen et al. 2019); Estonia built finance into teacher programmes |
| Teens already use cards, crypto, online shopping; **48%** offered "easy money" online | **Real-decision lessons:** scams, money mules, crypto, gambling odds, online loans | "Teachable moments" (Kaiser & Menkhoff 2017) |
| Knowledge and scam resilience fade over time | **Gamified, repeated practice** all year | Game-based RCT +0.31 SD (Cannistrà et al. 2024); prebunking fades without boosters (Burke et al.; BIT/OSC) |
| **73%** of pupils excluded from family money decisions | **Family tasks** | Family and school are complementary (Grohmann et al. 2015); parent spillovers (Bruhn et al. 2016; Frisancho) |
| The strategy counts events, not learning; no PISA financial literacy since 2018 | **Measurement:** 9th-grade survey every 2 years; PISA 2029; youth indicator | — |

## A3. Two-track advocacy

- **Insider track (cooperation).**
  - Join Latvijas Banka's working group (28 partners).
  - Co-design the module with VIAA.
  - Submit written proposals to IZM and the Interior Ministry's fraud plan.
  - Comment on draft regulations during public consultation on the TAP portal.
- **Outsider track (public pressure).**
  - Publish our evidence brief; place op-eds; give media interviews.
  - Collect student testimony and run scam-awareness content for teens.
  - Release pilot results publicly.
- **Why both:** insider work gives access and credibility. Outsider work raises political salience, which matters while the education budget is under pressure.
- **Rule:** never attack institutions. Frame it as a **system design gap** that everyone can fix together.

## A5. Pilot design (spring 2027): how we prove it works

- **Design:**
  - **10 pilot schools** deliver the module, taught by teachers who completed the course.
  - **10 matched comparison schools** continue as usual, matched on region, size, language and prior results.
  - Pupils take Latvijas Banka's 9th-grade instrument **before and after**.
- **What it can detect (honest power estimate):**
  - Assume ~50 pupils per school, intra-school correlation ~0.10, and a pre-test explaining ~50% of variance.
  - The pilot can reliably detect an effect of about **0.3 SD**.
  - That is enough to show feasibility and a meaningful effect, not a precise estimate.
- **For the national rollout:** roll out in waves by region (a **stepped-wedge** design). Each wave is compared with schools still waiting, using Latvijas Banka's biennial survey. This gives credible causal evidence at no extra data cost.
- **Also measure:**
  - teacher confidence (before and after);
  - delivery fidelity (lessons actually taught);
  - pupil and teacher satisfaction;
  - a new item: "if you received a money-mule offer, what would you do?"

## B2. How the base case is calculated (transparent assumptions)

- **Effect size:** school financial education raises knowledge by **+0.15 SD** (randomised-trial average) to **+0.33 SD** (all studies). For a full national rollout, where quality varies, we assume **+0.3 SD** as the base case.
- **Index:** we assume the 9th-grade index has a standard deviation of about **3 points**, based on its distribution (2% low, 61% medium, 37% high). So +0.3 SD ≈ **+0.9 points**, giving 12.3 → **≈ 13.2**. Reaching 14.0 needs about +0.55 SD, so it is a **stretch**.
- **Simple-interest item:** 57% correct corresponds to a position just above the middle of the distribution. Shifting everyone by +0.3 SD raises it to about **68%**. Reaching 75% needs about +0.5 SD.
- **Behaviour:** expect about **+0.07 to +0.09 SD** (meta-analyses), appearing over several years.
- **Delivery indicators** ("never learn at school", teachers trained) depend on **regulation and implementation**, not on effect sizes. They are the most controllable results.

## B3. Value for money: a break-even view (illustrative)

- **Programme cost:** about €0.2–0.35m set-up plus €0.1–0.2m a year. Spreading set-up over 5 years gives about **€0.15–0.27m a year**.
- **Average loss per successful fraud:** €7.83m across 2,829 cases in H1 2026, about **€2,770** per case.
- **Successful frauds a year:** about **5,700** (2 × 2,829).
- **Break-even:** the programme pays for itself in avoided losses if it prevents about **55–100 successful frauds a year**, around **1–2%** of all cases, before counting:
  - savings and debt benefits (Brazil's programme paid for itself through lower borrowing costs within ~5 years);
  - the human cost of money-mule criminal records.
- *This is an illustration, not a forecast. It shows the programme is cheap relative to the problem.*

## B4. Who benefits most (equity)

- Gaps are largest for pupils from **low-income families**, **basic education** households, and **Vidzeme and Kurzeme** schools (9th grade), and among **girls** (9th-grade knowledge and attitudes).
- School-based delivery reaches these groups better than voluntary events.
- **Ask:** prioritise these schools in rollout wave 1 and report KPIs by gender and region.

## C2. The proposals, with draft wording

*The draft wording is our proposal, for illustration, and would be refined with VIAA and lawyers.*

**1. Learning outcomes in the standards (Regulations No. 747 and No. 416)**

- **Change:** in the social and civic learning area (end of grade 9), add explicit outcomes. Example draft wording:

  > *LV:* "Skolēns izskaidro vienkāršo un salikto procentu, inflācijas, kā arī riska un ienesīguma ietekmi uz personīgajiem finanšu lēmumiem; atpazīst finanšu krāpšanas pazīmes, tostarp naudas mūļu vervēšanu, un zina, kā rīkoties un kur ziņot."
  >
  > *EN:* "The pupil explains how simple and compound interest, inflation, and risk and return affect personal financial decisions; recognises signs of financial fraud, including money-mule recruitment, and knows how to respond and where to report."

- Grades 10–12 (No. 416): add credit, investing, pensions and digital financial safety.
- **And in the sample programmes (annexes):** "no less than 10 lessons in grades 7–9 devoted to financial literacy within social studies".
- **Process:** IZM/VIAA draft → public consultation on TAP → announced at the State Secretaries' meeting → coordination → Cabinet. The annotation includes the impact assessment (cost: materials and training).
- **Timing:** draft in autumn 2027; adopted in 2028; applies from 1 September 2028 (new school year).

**2. Teacher training topic (Regulation No. 569)**

- **Change:** add "financial and digital financial literacy" to the professional competence topics for teachers of social studies and economics, within the existing **36 hours every 3 years**.
- **Precedent:** since **1 September 2025**, PE teachers who teach swimming must complete **24 hours** of swimming and water-safety training (point 20.1, Regulation No. 354 of 10.06.2025). The 2024 amendment only added sexual and reproductive health to the optional topic list.
- **Quick win (no regulation needed):** IZM/VIAA approve Latvijas Banka's seminar cycle "Finanšu pratība praksē" as a professional competence programme that counts towards the 36 hours.

**3. Education Development Guidelines 2028+ (Cabinet-approved)**

- **Change:** include "financial and digital financial literacy" among the target skills, with an indicator (9th-grade index; PISA financial literacy) and a funding line for teacher training.
- **Why this matters:** it is the document that steers the 2028–2031 budget frame and the 2028–2034 EU funds.
- **Timing:** comment during drafting and public consultation (2027).

**4. National Financial Literacy Strategy 2028+ (partnership)**

- **Change:** a **school pillar** with **youth outcome targets** (not activity counts), linked to the OECD action plan (November 2026) and the fraud-prevention plan.
- **Optional:** ask that the strategy be **endorsed by the Cabinet**, or linked to the National Development Plan, to give it weight.

**5. PISA 2029 financial literacy option (IZM decision)**

- **Change:** commit and budget the participation fee.
- **Why:** it is the only international benchmark for 15-year-olds, and Latvia skipped it in 2022.

**6. Supporting changes**

- **Fraud-prevention plan:** add "school-based financial and digital literacy, including money-mule awareness" as a prevention measure. Finance Latvia's 17 proposals do not cover schools.
- **Gambling advertising (Gambling and Lotteries Law):** tighten youth-attractive advertising, as the Ministry of Finance/PwC study recommends. This is a longer route through the Saeima.
- **Grade 9 assessment:** include financial literacy items in the state social studies assessment, so what is assessed gets taught. This is a later step.

## C4. Likely objections and answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "It's already in the curriculum" | Outcomes are general, there are no minimum lessons, and 36% of pupils say it never happens. England had the same issue and changed course in 2025 |
| "Teachers are overloaded" | No new subject; existing hours and the existing 36-hour training; ready-made materials |
| "Why regulate instead of running projects?" | 230+ activities since 2021, yet knowledge fell. Only rules make it reach every pupil |
| "Banks will push products" | VIAA approves the content; volunteers follow a code of conduct; no product promotion |
| "What does it cost?" | About €0.15–0.27m a year, about 1% of a year's fraud losses; it breaks even if it prevents ~55–100 frauds a year |
| "Will it change behaviour?" | Knowledge effects are proven. Behaviour effects are smaller and slower, so we measure them over years and add repetition and family tasks |

## C5. EU alignment

- **Commission Financial Literacy Strategy** (30.09.2025): asks member states to embed financial literacy, starting in schools; EU/OECD competence frameworks; a new Eurobarometer in 2027.
- **European Parliament resolution** (30.04.2026, 502–46–42): integrate financial education into school curricula; minimum standards for finfluencers; the Commission to report by the end of 2027.
- **Payment Services Regulation** (agreed 27.11.2025): payment providers are liable for weak fraud prevention, which strengthens banks' incentive to co-fund education.
