# New data and regulatory levers (November 2026)

Research note that sits behind the presentation. *[verify]* = from press or summaries, so check before quoting.

## New data

**OECD and Latvijas Banka fraud survey** (Kantar, n = 1,400, ages 18–79, presented 20.11.2026)
- 74% of adults faced a fraud attempt in the past two years; 27% became victims.
- 55% of victims lost money; 37% told no one, most often because "it would change nothing" (57%).
- Most common types: purchase fraud 15%, phishing 8%, investment fraud 6.5%, unauthorised transactions 5.6%.
- The OECD report proposes a digital financial literacy action plan for Latvia. Latvijas Banka will coordinate it through the 28-partner working group.

**Fraud losses (Finance Latvia Association member data)**
- In the first half of 2026, financial institutions prevented 10,960 attempts worth €11.0m, but 2,829 succeeded and residents lost **€7.83m**.
- Phone fraud accounted for €4.26m of those losses, across 1,333 cases.
- For 2025, the State Police reports total fraud losses of **€23.7m**, up from €16m in 2024 *[verify]*.

**Money mules.** In one phone-fraud case, the police identified **170+ money mules** and illegal crypto exchangers (prosecutor's office, 18.03.2026).

**PISA 2025** (published 08.09.2026)
- Latvia's results in mathematics, reading and science were the **lowest since it joined PISA in 2000**, and below the OECD average in every domain.
- Only 2.6% of students reach the top level (OECD average 5.8%).
- No PISA 2025 financial literacy results for Latvia were found. The next option is **PISA 2029**.

**EU monitoring.** No new Eurobarometer since 2023. The next Flash Eurobarometer on financial literacy is planned for **2027**.

## Regulatory levers (for slide 12)

| Instrument | Status | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Cabinet Regulation No. 747 (basic education standard, in force 01.09.2020) and No. 416 (general secondary standard) | Financial literacy is a topic inside social studies; no minimum lessons | Explicit, assessed outcomes (interest, inflation, risk, digital fraud) and a minimum block of lessons |
| Cabinet Regulation No. 569 (teacher education and training): **36 hours per 3 years** | **2024 amendment added sexual and reproductive health as a topic**. This is a precedent | Add financial and digital financial literacy as a required topic for social-studies teachers |
| National Financial Literacy Strategy 2021–2027 | Ends 2027; no public successor draft | Strategy for 2028 onwards with youth outcome targets and a school pillar |
| Government fraud-prevention plan (Interior Ministry; proposals due 31.07.2026) | In preparation; a Saeima committee noted there is no single coordinating body | Include financial education in schools as a prevention measure |
| PISA participation (IZM) | Latvia skipped financial literacy in 2022 | Join the PISA 2029 financial literacy option |
| EU Payment Services Regulation (provisional agreement 27.11.2025) | Payment providers liable for weak fraud prevention; refunds for bank-impersonation fraud; platforms liable to payment providers | An argument for banks to co-fund education |
| EU Financial Literacy Strategy, COM(2025) 681 (30.09.2025) | Member states encouraged to act, starting in schools; review in 2027 | EU backing for the proposal |
