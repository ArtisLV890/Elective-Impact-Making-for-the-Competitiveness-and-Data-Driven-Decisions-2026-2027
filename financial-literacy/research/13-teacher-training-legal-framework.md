# Bottom line

1. **Today, teachers choose their own training.**
   - Every teacher must complete **at least 36 hours of professional development in 3 years**, planned together with the school head.
   - The regulation lists only **broad topic areas**: general teaching competences, subject content and teaching methods, and school management.
   - The **only topic required of every teacher is child-rights protection (at least 6 hours)**.
   - Nothing requires a social-studies teacher to take any finance training.
2. **The government can require it, and there is a direct precedent.**
   - The Cabinet sets the training rules (Education Law, Art. 14 point 32), so a requirement needs only an **amendment to Regulation No. 569**, not a new law.
   - **Since 1 September 2025, PE teachers who teach swimming must complete 24 hours of training** in swimming instruction and water safety (point 20.1). The justification was safety: drowning.
   - That is exactly the model we need: **a required course for teachers of one subject, justified by a risk to young people**. Here the risks are financial fraud and money mules.
3. **It can sit inside the existing 36 hours**, so it adds no extra burden.
   - A 12-hour finance module every 3 years would be a third of the hours teachers already owe.
4. **Who can run the course matters.**
   - Latvijas Banka is independent, not subordinate to a ministry, so it is **not one of the bodies the regulation names as course providers**.
   - Its content can still count if the course is delivered through one of the named routes:
     - **VIAA** (as an agency subordinate to the Ministry of Education);
     - **a university with teacher-training programmes**;
     - **a municipality or an NGO, with the municipality's approval**.
5. **The subject has very little time.**
   - In grades 7–9, social studies gets **105 hours over three years: about one lesson a week**, shared with politics, law, ethics and economics.
   - So "10–12 finance lessons a year" is not realistic inside social studies alone. A realistic ask is **about 10–12 finance lessons across grades 7–9 in total, plus more in secondary school**, backed by maths.

# 2. What the law requires of teachers today

## 2.1 The 36-hour rule (Regulation No. 569)

**Point 15**

- General-education teachers are **responsible for their own professional development**.
- They must complete a programme of **at least 36 hours within 3 years**, **planned together with the school head**.

**Point 18: the programme must cover one of three broad areas**

1. **General teaching competences.** Innovations in education, upbringing, civic attitudes, inclusive education, sustainable development, **child-rights protection, health and safety, recognising violence, sexual and reproductive health**.
2. **Content and didactics.** Competence-based teaching, literacy, digital skills, **innovations in the subject's content and methods**.
3. **School management.** Leadership, quality monitoring, HR, and **"financial skills"**, meaning school finance management, not personal finance.

**So a finance course for teachers would count today under area 2** ("innovations in the subject's content and methods"). But nothing makes anyone take it.

**Other rules**

- **Point 19:** the school head can credit **at most 12 hours** in 3 years from conferences, seminars, master classes, company internships and similar.
- **Point 19.1:** foreign programmes and Erasmus+ can be credited in full.
- **Point 17:** newly qualified teachers start the 36-hour cycle in their third year.

## 2.2 What is actually required

| Requirement | Who | Hours | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child-rights protection | **All teachers** | **At least 6 hours** | Reg. 569, point 20; Child Rights Protection Law, Art. 5.1. Detailed rules now in Cabinet Regulation No. 391 (07.07.2026) **[verify the new details]** |
| Swimming instruction and water safety | **PE teachers who teach swimming** | **24 hours**, plus first aid, plus 4 hours of water safety every 5 years | Reg. 569, point 20.1 (Regulation No. 354, 10.06.2025, in force 01.09.2025) |
| Everything else | All teachers | Free choice within 36 hours | Reg. 569, points 15 and 18 |

**Correction to our earlier materials.**

- The 22.10.2024 amendment (No. 654) only **added sexual and reproductive health to the list of optional topics** and set up a programme teachers *can* take. It did not make it mandatory.
- The real precedent for a mandatory, subject-specific course is the **2025 swimming rule**.

## 2.3 Teachers' rights and duties (Education Law)

- **Art. 51(1) point 4:** a teacher's duty is to "improve their professional competence".
- **Art. 52(1) point 4:** a teacher may use **30 calendar days in 3 years, on full pay**, for their own education and development.
- **Art. 52(1) point 4.1:** teachers have the right to **support from the school head and the school's owner** for their development.
- **Art. 49(3):** development happens through **self-study and courses** run by institutions offering continuing education.

## 2.4 Who can run an approved course (Reg. 569, point 21)

| Route | Who | Approval | Fits financial literacy? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21.1** | Municipal or private school; **NGO whose statutes include teacher training** | **Approved by the municipality** where the course runs | **Yes.** An NGO such as Jaunie Ekonomisti could qualify if its statutes cover teacher training |
| **21.2** | Institutions subordinate to ministries, or municipal institutions with education in their bylaws (for example **VIAA**, municipal education boards); **universities with teacher-training programmes** | No extra approval named | **Yes, the strongest route.** Latvijas Banka content delivered with VIAA or a university (for example LU, LiepU, DU) |
| 21.3 / 21.4 | Universities: 72-hour or 160-hour certificate programmes | Agreed with IZM | For teachers retraining to teach economics; not needed here |
| 21.5 | Foreign institutions in Latvia | Agreed with IZM | Not relevant |

- **After the course,** the provider issues a certificate of completion (*apliecība*, Annex 1). It states whether a municipality approved the programme.
- **Latvijas Banka is independent and not subordinate to a ministry,** so on our reading it is not itself a 21.2 provider.
- **Whether its seminar cycle "Finanšu pratība praksē" (2026, 12 topics over 3 years) currently gives a certificate that counts toward the 36 hours: [verify with Latvijas Banka].** If it does not, partnering with VIAA or a university is the fix.

# 3. Can teachers be required to do it?

## 3.1 Option A: change nothing, encourage only (status quo)

- Teachers and heads *may* choose finance training.
- **Peru shows what this produces:** only 43% attended all the training, and a third of teachers never taught the lessons. We do not recommend relying on this.

## 3.2 Option B: soft steering without changing the regulation

| Lever | Who | How | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal training plans | Municipal education boards (Art. 17) | Make financial literacy a priority topic in the municipality's teacher-development plan | Medium, but varies by municipality |
| State support project | VIAA (ESF+ 4.2.2.3) | Fund and run a financial-literacy course for social-studies and maths teachers within the existing project | Medium. Funding ends 2026; check the next period **[verify]** |
| Course approval | VIAA, a university or a municipality | Make sure Latvijas Banka's seminars give a certificate that counts toward the 36 hours | Low cost, quick |
| School quality grades | School head (Education Law Art. 49.1, 53(2); Reg. 501) | Schools may count finance training in their criteria for the voluntary quality grade, which carries a salary bonus | Weak. Each school writes its own criteria |
| School priorities | School head (Reg. 569, point 15) | The head plans training with each teacher and can prioritise it | Depends on each head |

## 3.3 Option C: a required course (recommended, using the swimming precedent)

- **Add a new point to Regulation No. 569**, modelled on point 20.1. **Illustrative wording, to be refined with lawyers:**

> **20.2** Vispārējās izglītības pedagogs, kas māca sociālās zinības pamatizglītības 7.–9. klasē vai sociālo zinātņu, ekonomikas vai uzņēmējdarbības kursus vidējā izglītībā, triju gadu laikā pilnveido profesionālo kompetenci finanšu pratībā, tai skaitā digitālajā finanšu drošībā, vismaz 12 stundu apjomā šo noteikumu 21. punktā norādītajās programmās.

- **In English:** teachers of social studies in grades 7–9, and of social sciences, economics or business in secondary school, complete **at least 12 hours of financial literacy training, including digital financial safety, every 3 years**, within approved programmes.

**Why this design**

- **It targets only the teachers who teach the topic.** That is a few hundred to about a thousand teachers **[verify the number with IZM]**, not all 25,000+.
- **It fits inside the existing 36 hours:** no extra workload, no extra days.
- **It follows the 2025 swimming precedent:** subject-specific, justified by a risk to students.
  - Swimming: drowning.
  - Finance: fraud, money mules, debt.
  - The OECD (November 2026) calls school financial literacy "essential".
- **12 hours** is about 2 seminar days, in line with the evidence on effective teacher training (Peru: 20 hours).

**The process**

1. IZM drafts the amendment.
2. Public consultation on the TAP portal.
3. Announcement at the State Secretaries' meeting.
4. Cabinet adoption.
5. Apply from a start date, for example 1 September 2028, with a transition period.
6. **Make sure courses exist first**, as was done for swimming and sexual-health training: Latvijas Banka content, delivered via VIAA or universities.

## 3.4 Option D: longer term, new teachers

- Include financial literacy in **teacher-training degrees** for social-studies and maths teachers.
- This works through universities and the teacher profession standard, not Regulation No. 569 **[verify the exact instrument]**.

# 4. The curriculum: how much time is there?

## 4.1 Basic education (Regulation No. 747)

**Hours** (sample study plan, as amended 2026)

- **Social studies, grades 7–9: 105 hours over 3 years**, about 1 lesson a week.
- Grades 4–6: "social studies and history" together, 245 hours.
- Grades 7–9 also: Latvian and world history, 210 hours.

**Finance outcomes students must reach by the end of grade 9** (Annex 3, social and civic area):

- **5.1** Explain that limited resources mean households, businesses and the state must make choices.
- **5.2** Analyse how the state budget is formed and why taxes must be paid; explain **inflation and the role of central banks**.
- **5.4** "Make **responsible decisions by evaluating the benefits of services offered by various financial institutions**; analyse the link between the financial resources needed and the needs and values that determine the possibility of **deposits and borrowing**."
- **4.3** Practical business experience, and calculating income, expenses and profit.

**What is missing:**

- **interest and compound interest;**
- **fraud, digital financial safety and money mules;**
- **investing and pensions;**
- consumer credit and BNPL.

The standard lists outcomes, not hours. How much time finance gets is left to the sample programme and the teacher.

## 4.2 Secondary education (Regulation No. 416)

The secondary standard already has **detailed finance outcomes**, for example:

- manage a personal budget and avoid overspending;
- compare ways of using and investing money, the price of financial services and opportunity costs;
- explain what affects the value of money;
- distinguish financial markets: banks, loans and savings, shares, bonds, **peer-to-peer lending**;
- identify risks to one's money and property;
- compare consumer-protection rules.

Which course and which level these sit in should be checked **[verify]**. Secondary maths also uses financial examples (loans, taxes, rent).

## 4.3 What this means for our proposal

- **Correct the "10–12 lessons per grade" claim.** With one social-studies lesson a week, 10–12 finance lessons in a single year would be a third of the subject. A realistic minimum:
  - **grades 7–9:** about **10–12 finance lessons in total** (about 10% of social-studies time), spread over the three years;
  - **maths:** interest, percentages and loan examples. The standard already allows this, and the US evidence (Cole et al.) shows maths matters;
  - **secondary:** the existing detailed outcomes, delivered in the social-sciences and economics courses.
- **Add the missing outcomes to Regulation No. 747:**
  - compound interest;
  - fraud and digital financial safety;
  - money mules;
  - saving and investing basics.
- **The curriculum is being changed right now.** Regulation No. 747 was amended in April and September 2026 (Nos. 221 and 540), and the ministry is considering rebuilding the social-studies course. That is the window.
