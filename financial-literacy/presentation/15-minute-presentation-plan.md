# The one sentence to remember

> **Financial literacy is compulsory in every Latvian school, yet young Latvians are among the least financially literate in the EU, while scammers recruit them online. We propose four ideas to make it real: training that counts, required training, teaching within the standard, and measurement, needing one new point in a Cabinet regulation and no new law.**

**Three messages the audience must leave with:**

1. **The problem is real and urgent.** Latvia is joint last in the EU, knowledge is falling, and teenagers are targets.
2. **We know what works.** Teaching by trained teachers, built into existing courses (Peru), not one-off events or mandates on paper.
3. **The fix is doable now.** Four concrete ideas, one small rule change, and the policy window is open until 2027.

# Part 1. Opening (1:00), slides 1–2

- **Open with the paradox:** "Financial literacy has been compulsory in every Latvian school for years. So why do 36% of 9th graders say they never learn about money at school?"
- **Or open with a story:** a 16-year-old gets a Telegram message: "€200 a week, just lend us your bank account." 18% of 9th graders have received an offer like this.
- **Slide 2:** the three messages and the five questions we answer.

# Part 2. (i) The issue (3:30), slides 3–12

**Latvia is last** (slides 4–5)

- **11%** of adults have high financial literacy, joint last in the EU (EU 18%).
- **26%** have no emergency savings, the highest in the EU.

**The young are weakest, and knowledge is falling** (slides 6–8)

- 18–19-year-olds score **8.0 out of 20**, against **12.2** for all adults.
- Knowledge fell from **5.1 to 3.9 out of 7** between 2015 and 2022.
- 15-year-olds score **12.3/20**, no better than adults.

**School is where they learn least** (slide 9)

- **36%** never learn about money at school.
- Only **9%** learn about it there often.

**Teenagers are targets** (slides 10–11)

- **48%** have been offered "easy money" online.
- **18%** have been asked to be a money mule.
- Fraud losses rose from **€8.5m to €23.7m** in two years.

**Why now** (slide 12)

- Every plan that matters is being written in 2027: the national strategy, the education guidelines, the OECD plan, and the social-studies course redesign.
- *Transition:* "So what actually works?"

# Part 3. (ii) Research and data (2:30), slides 13–18

**Slide 14: what works**

- School financial education raises knowledge by **+0.15 to +0.33 SD** (meta-analyses).
- **A mandate on paper fails** (US, Cole et al. 2016). Real courses work (Urban et al. 2020).

**Slide 15: Peru**, the strongest slide in the section

- Peru put finance lessons inside its existing social-science course, with workbooks and 20 hours of teacher training.
- **Results:**
  - students **+0.16 SD**;
  - teachers **+0.32 SD**, and **+0.46 SD** for teachers who actually taught;
  - 15 points less likely to be in debt, where pupils were taught twice;
  - **US$4.8 per pupil**.
- **The warning:** a third of teachers taught nothing, because it was voluntary.
- *Say:* "Peru shows both what works and what fails. Our four ideas copy the first and fix the second."

**Slides 16–18:** Latvia has lots of activity but no results; other countries' models; scam education must be repeated. Use them as backup, or skip them if short of time.

# Part 4. (iii) Strategy (3:30), slides 19–24

**Slide 20: the four ideas** (1:00)

1. **Training that counts:** Latvijas Banka's seminars run through VIAA or a university, so they count in full toward teachers' 36 hours.
2. **Required training:** 12 of the 36 hours in financial literacy, for social-studies teachers, on the model of the 2025 swimming rule.
3. **Teach it within the standard:** the existing outcomes already allow it; VIAA adds a finance thread, lessons and test questions.
4. **Measure and show:** the 9th-grade survey every 2 years, the PISA 2029 finance module, and a yearly scorecard.

- *Say:* "No new law, no company money, one new point in a Cabinet regulation."

**Slide 21: idea 1, the legal context** (0:50)

- **Today:** Latvijas Banka's sessions are legally "seminars", capped at 12 hours in 3 years. A teacher who attends everything may get 12 hours or fewer counted.
- **After:** delivered by VIAA or a university, all the hours count.

**Slide 22: idea 3** (0:40)

- Outcomes 5.4 and 3.3 and the maths percentage outcomes already cover credit, phishing and compound interest.
- VIAA adds what is missing: a finance thread in the programmes, ready lessons and test questions.

**Slides 23–24:** who we work with, and the roadmap to 2031 (0:40).

# Part 5. (iv) Expected results (1:30), slides 25–27

**Say it as three dates:**

| Year | What exists | How we will know |
|---|---|---|
| 2028 | Point 20.2 adopted; Latvijas Banka course runs through VIAA; finance lessons and test questions published | Cabinet decision; VIAA course list |
| 2029 | First new data: Latvijas Banka survey (also 2027); PISA 2029 finance module | Published results |
| 2031 | All social-studies teachers have the 12 hours; "never learn about money at school" 36% → under 20%; 9th-grade index 12.3 → about 13 | Latvijas Banka survey 2031; VIAA certificates |

The targets are modest on purpose: they assume a gain the size of Peru's (about +0.2 SD).

**Cost (slide 27):**

- Most of it sits inside existing budgets and duties.
- The only clearly new spending is the PISA module fee.
- For scale: €23.7m was lost to fraud in 2025, and Peru spent US$4.8 per pupil.

# Part 6. (v) Regulation (2:00), slides 28–32

**Slide 29: what changes and what does not**

- **One new rule:** Regulation No. 569, point 20.2.
- **The standards stay as they are.**
- **Commitments:** in the Education Development Guidelines and the 2028+ strategy.

**Slide 30: the draft rule** (1:00)

- Show the Latvian text, then the plain-words box and the 36-hour bar: 6 hours child rights, **12 hours financial literacy**, 18 hours free choice.
- *Say:* "We don't ask teachers to do more. We ask the teachers who teach money to spend part of their existing training on money, just as PE teachers who teach swimming must learn water safety."

**Slide 32: how it happens**

1. IZM drafts the amendment.
2. Public consultation on TAP.
3. Coordination, including local governments, the unions (LIZDA) and employers.
4. The Cabinet adopts it. It applies from 1 September 2028, with compliance by 2031.

**Slide 31:** the OECD's new plan calls school financial literacy "essential". Use it if there is time, or in Q&A.

# Part 7. Close (1:00), slide 33

**Three asks:**

- **IZM:** adopt point 20.2 and opt into the PISA 2029 finance module.
- **VIAA:** run Latvijas Banka's seminars as an approved course, and add a finance thread to programmes and tests.
- **Latvijas Banka:** repeat the 9th-grade survey every 2 years, and give schools a pillar in the 2028+ strategy.

**Last line:** "We already made financial literacy compulsory. Now let's make it real, before the scammers teach our teenagers first."

# Likely questions and short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Isn't it already compulsory?" | On paper, yes. But teachers choose their own training, social studies has about one lesson a week, and 36% of pupils say it never happens |
| "Can the government require a training topic?" | Yes. The Cabinet sets training rules (Education Law, Art. 14). It already requires child-rights training for all teachers and, since 2025, 24 hours of swimming training for PE teachers who teach swimming |
| "Teachers are overloaded." | The 12 hours sit inside the 36 they already owe: no extra hours or days. Courses are free and online |
| "Why don't Latvijas Banka's seminars count now?" | Latvijas Banka is not a course provider under the rules, so its sessions count as seminars, capped at 12 hours. Delivered through VIAA or a university, all the hours count |
| "Why not change the curriculum standard?" | It's slow and full. The existing outcomes already cover credit, phishing and compound interest. What's missing is guidance, lessons and test questions, which VIAA can add itself |
| "Aren't we already in PISA?" | Yes, for reading, maths and science. Financial literacy is an optional module. Latvia took it in 2012 and 2018 but skipped 2022. We ask to opt in again in 2029 |
| "Does it change behaviour?" | Knowledge effects are solid. In Peru, pupils taught more had less debt three years later. Behaviour changes more slowly, which is why we measure every 2 years |
| "Who pays?" | Mostly existing structures: Latvijas Banka's content and VIAA's teacher-support role. The PISA module needs a line in IZM's budget |
| "What's your NGO's role?" | Evidence, draft wording, an outcome map for VIAA, the yearly scorecard, and the youth voice. We are not asking to run the system |
