# The one sentence to remember

> **Financial literacy is compulsory in every Latvian school, yet young Latvians are the least financially literate group in one of the least financially literate countries in the EU, while scammers recruit them online. We propose to make it real through teacher training, a ready-made module, regular practice and four regulation changes.**

**Three messages the audience must leave with**

1. **The problem is real and urgent.** Latvia is last in the EU, knowledge is falling, and young people are targets.
2. **We know what works.** Intensive teaching by trained teachers, not one-off events or mandates on paper.
3. **The fix is cheap and doable now.** Cabinet-level changes, no new law, and the policy window is open until 2027.

# Part 1. Opening hook (1:00), slide 1

- **Open with the paradox:** "Financial literacy has been compulsory in every Latvian school since the Skola2030 reform. So why do 36% of 9th graders say they have never learned about money at school?"
- **Or open with a story:** a 16-year-old gets a Telegram message: "€200 a week, just lend us your bank account." 18% of 9th graders have received an offer like this.
- Introduce yourselves in one line: Jaunie Ekonomisti, a youth NGO that already runs an economics Olympiad (about 200 students from 30+ schools).

# Part 2. (i) The issue (3:30)

**Slide 2: Latvia is joint last in the EU (1:00)**

- **11%** of adults have high financial literacy: joint last in the EU (EU 18%, Estonia 23%).
- **26%** have no emergency savings: the highest in the EU.
- **Only 46%** reach the OECD minimum score (OECD average 58%).
- *Say:* "It's not just knowledge. We are 26th of 27 on financial behaviour."

**Slide 3: Young people are the weakest, and knowledge is falling (0:50)**

- Adults' knowledge score fell from **5.1 to 3.9 out of 7** (2015 → 2022). Compound interest: 48% → 26% correct.
- 18–19-year-olds: **8.0 out of 20**, against 12.2 for all adults. Only 40% can calculate simple interest.

**Slide 4: School is where they learn least (0:50)**

- 9th graders: **36% never** learn about money at school, and **9%** often. The internet and family dominate.
- Financial literacy is **one topic inside social studies**, with **no minimum lessons**, taught "superficially" (LSM 2026).
- **Teachers' own knowledge of interest is insufficient** (Latvijas Banka, 2024).

**Slide 5: Why now: fraud (0:50)**

- **74%** of adults faced a fraud attempt in two years, and **27%** became victims (OECD, November 2026).
- **€7.8m** was lost in the first half of 2026 alone.
- Teens: **48%** offered "easy money" online; **18%** asked to be money mules; **29%** of 15–16-year-olds gambled for money.
- *Transition:* "So what does research say actually works?"

# Part 3. (ii) Research and data (2:30)

**Slide 6: What works (1:30)**

- School financial education raises knowledge by **+0.15 to +0.33 SD** (meta-analysis of 37 studies; 76 RCTs confirm it).
- Large trials: Brazil (892 schools) **+0.25 SD**; Peru (300 schools) better credit records 3 years later; an online game **+0.31 SD**.
- **Four lessons:**
  1. intensity matters;
  2. teachers matter;
  3. link lessons to real decisions;
  4. **a mandate on paper fails**: earlier US mandates had no effect.
- Scam education works, but **fades within days or months** unless repeated. One campaign is not enough.

**Slide 7: Latvia has activity but not results (1:00)**

- 230+ strategy activities since 2021; Money Day reached 78% of schools. Yet knowledge fell.
- Only **~9%** of activities target teachers.
- **England** is the warning: compulsory since 2014, yet only 38% of young people recall it, so it is being strengthened (2025).
- **Estonia** is the model: PISA 547, best in the world, with financial literacy built into teacher training.
- *Say:* "Latvia doesn't need more events. It needs intensive teaching by trained teachers."

# Part 4. (iii) Strategy (3:30)

**Slide 8: Five steps (1:30)**

1. **Module:** 10–12 ready-to-teach lessons in grades 9 and 11, each tied to a real decision (scams, money mules, crypto and finfluencers, online loans, the first salary and the pension pillar).
2. **Teacher training:** build on Latvijas Banka's new seminar cycle (2026); certify it so it counts within teachers' required 36 hours.
3. **Gamified practice** through the year: Olympiad, escape-room games, bank volunteers as "finance coaches". *(This is where our NGO already has a working format.)*
4. **Family tasks:** 73% of pupils are left out of money decisions at home.
5. **Measure:** Latvijas Banka's 9th-grade survey every 2 years; join PISA 2029.

**Slide 9: Who we work with (1:00)** (one line each)

- **Policymakers:** IZM and VISC (standards and training), Saeima committees, the Interior Ministry fraud plan.
- **Central bank and industry:** Latvijas Banka's 28-partner coalition; banks and Finance Latvia, which now have a financial interest in fraud prevention (EU payment rules).
- **Schools and parents:** a pilot in 10 schools.
- **Media:** LSM, Delfi and TVNet; Financial Literacy Week in March; teen-facing social media.

**Slide 10: Roadmap (1:00)**

- **Now to Feb 2027:** evidence brief and meetings.
- **March 2027:** launch at Financial Literacy Week.
- **Spring 2027:** pilot in 10 schools.
- **Autumn 2027:** proposals into the new strategy and regulations.
- **2028–2030:** rollout and PISA 2029.

# Part 5. (iv) Expected results (1:30), slide 11

**Pick three KPIs to say out loud:**

| KPI | Baseline | Target 2030 |
|---|---|---|
| 9th-grade index | 12.3 | 14.0 |
| Simple-interest skill | 57% | 75% |
| "Never learn about money at school" | 36% | under 10% |

**Cost:** about **€0.2–0.35m to set up** and **€0.1–0.2m a year**. That's **about 1% of one year's fraud losses**, and Brazil's programme paid for itself in about 5 years.

# Part 6. (v) Regulation (2:00), slide 12

**Four changes, and none needs a new law:**

1. **Education standards** (Cabinet Regulations No. 747 and 416): explicit, assessed financial literacy outcomes and a **minimum block of lessons**.
2. **Teacher training rules** (No. 569, 36 hours every 3 years): make financial and digital literacy a required topic. **Precedent:** a new required topic was added in 2024.
3. **National strategy 2028+:** a school pillar with youth targets, linked to the OECD action plan and the fraud-prevention plan.
4. **Join PISA 2029** financial literacy.

**EU tailwind:** the Commission's Financial Literacy Strategy (2025), and the European Parliament resolution (April 2026) calling for financial education in school curricula.

# Part 7. Close (1:00), slide 13

**Three asks:**
- **IZM and VISC:** put outcomes, minimum lessons and teacher training into regulation.
- **Latvijas Banka:** a school pillar with youth targets in the next strategy.
- **Banks and Finance Latvia:** co-fund teacher training and send volunteer coaches.

**Last line:** "We already made financial literacy compulsory. Now let's make it real, before the scammers teach our teenagers first."

# Likely questions and short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Isn't it already compulsory?" | On paper, yes. But there are no minimum lessons, teachers are under-prepared, and 36% of pupils say it never happens. England had the same problem and is now fixing it |
| "Does financial education change behaviour?" | Knowledge effects are solid; behaviour effects are smaller (~+0.07 SD) and appear over years. That's why we start with knowledge KPIs and add repetition and family tasks |
| "Teachers are already overloaded" | We use existing hours and existing required training (36 hours), and give ready-made materials, building on Latvijas Banka's seminar cycle |
| "Who pays?" | IZM for training, Latvijas Banka for content, banks and Finance Latvia for co-funding (they now carry fraud liability), and EU funds |
| "Why schools and not adults?" | School is the only place that reaches every young person. The OECD's own plan for Latvia (Nov 2026) says integrating digital financial literacy into primary and secondary education is "essential" (Action 4), but gives it no owner or timeline. We fill that gap |
| "Doesn't the OECD say middle-aged adults are the main victims?" | Yes: 35–64-year-olds are more likely victims than 18–24-year-olds. Schools are prevention: build habits before people bank, shop and invest online at scale. And teenagers are already targeted: 48% offered "easy money", 18% asked to be money mules |
| "Aren't your 18–19 numbers based on a small sample?" | Yes, about 30 people. But 20–29-year-olds (11.0) and the EU-wide data show the same pattern |
| "What's your NGO's role?" | Evidence, the youth voice, and a working gamified format (the Olympiad). We are not asking to run the system |

# Delivery tips

- **One message per slide**, said in the first sentence. The rest is support.
- **Use the chart, don't read it:** point to one bar ("this is us, last").
- **Say sources briefly:** "Latvijas Banka", "OECD", "Eurobarometer". It builds credibility.
- **Rehearse the transitions:** problem → "what works?" → "so here's our plan" → "what it achieves" → "what must change in law".
- **Keep one minute in reserve.** If time runs short, cut slide 7 and slide 10 first.
