# Five similar universities, one Mile: is overlap a dealbreaker, and what can be done?

Dedicated research note, 9 November 2026.

## 1. The question and the bottom line

**Question:** The five Knowledge Mile universities (LU, RTU, RSU, RISEBA, BA Turība) offer largely the same things. If so, can a Knowledge Mile work at all, and what are the options?

**Bottom line:**
1. **The overlap is real, but it is concentrated in teaching**, mainly business and management, law, communication and health management. The research cores still differ: engineering at RTU, medicine at RSU, broad sciences at LU, architecture and media at RISEBA, tourism at Turība.
2. **Overlap does not rule out a Knowledge Mile, but it changes what the Mile can be.** It cannot be sold as "five complementary institutions" that naturally collaborate. Similar universities compete for the same students and state-funded places, which helps explain why the 2024 memorandum stalled.
3. **Abroad, similar neighbouring institutions solve this in one of four ways:**
   - share services and courses (consortia);
   - build joint centres around their *differences*;
   - agree on a division of labour (profiling);
   - merge.
4. **Our recommendation is a sequence:**
   - **Now:** cheap coordination that works despite the overlap (coordinator, cross-registration, shared services).
   - **Next:** joint "missions" built on the existing differences.
   - **Later:** a profiling agreement backed by state funding incentives.
   - A merger is the expensive last resort, and it is impossible for the two private universities.

*✓ = present; "core" = the institution's main identity; – = not found in public sources.*

**What this shows:**
- **Business and management is taught at all five.** Law is at three, communication at four, and medicine at two (LU and RSU).
- **The trend is towards more overlap, not less.** RTU is expanding Riga Business School (2026), RISEBA teaches health management, and LU absorbed a business school (2025).
- **Each university still has a distinct core** that the others don't have: RTU's engineering, RSU's medicine and health professions, RISEBA's architecture and audiovisual arts, and Turība's tourism.
- **Infrastructure is duplicated too.** LaSER (2026) reports that LU plans a 15,000+ m² sports and health building 1.5 km from RSU's new sports complex, with no joint assessment.

**Why it happens (likely mechanisms, to be confirmed):**
- Funding follows students, through state-funded study places and tuition fees. So every institution has an incentive to offer popular, cheap-to-teach programmes such as business and law.
- The Ministry of Education has noted that institutions with more state-funded places show more fragmentation (LSM).
- The Ministry of Finance and FICIL have both criticised the "excessive duplication" of programmes relative to student numbers.

## 3. What theory says

- **Boschma (2005):** too much cognitive proximity means lock-in and little new to learn.
- **Nooteboom (2000):** there is an optimal cognitive distance, close enough to understand each other and different enough to learn. Broekel & Boschma (2012) find empirical support for such an optimum.
- **Frenken, Van Oort & Verburg (2007):** regions benefit from **related variety**, activities that are different but connected.
- **Our own inference:** where offers overlap, the relationship is mostly **competition** (for students, places, staff). Competition lowers trust, which is social proximity in Boschma's terms. So overlap hurts in two ways: there is less to learn from each other, and less willingness to try.
- **But overlap is not all bad.** Boudreau et al. (2017) found more collaboration *within one institution*, among people with similar backgrounds. Similar people can still find partners once search costs fall.

## 4. What happens elsewhere

| Pattern | Examples | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **One anchor** (most common) | Kendall Square (MIT), Otaniemi (Aalto), Eindhoven (TU/e) | The district grows around one dominant university. Riga doesn't have one. |
| **Several universities with different profiles** | Pittsburgh (Pitt: medicine; CMU: computing; UPMC), Montreal QI (ÉTS engineering + McGill, "complementary strengths"), Manchester (research + applied university + hospitals), Melbourne (University of Melbourne + RMIT) | Districts are built on what is different, and each partner has a clear role. |
| **Similar institutions side by side: consortium** | Colleges of the Fenway, Boston (5 colleges: up to 2 courses a semester elsewhere, 300–400 students a semester); Longwood/MASCO, Boston (joint body since 1972 on land use, transport and "duplication of services") | Overlap can be turned into shared choice and shared services. |
| **Similar institutions: merger** | Strasbourg (2009), Grenoble Alpes, Manchester + UMIST (2004), Paris-Saclay; in Latvia, Banku augstskola into LU (2025) and Liepāja University into RTU (2023) | Removes duplication but is slow, costly and can create "cumbersome" management. |
| **State-led profiling** | Netherlands sector plans (national agreements on focal areas per university, about €60m a year from 2018); Finland PROFI (e.g. €100m to 9 universities in 2023 to sharpen research profiles) | Profiling happens when the state pays for it. Universities don't specialise voluntarily against their revenue. |

## 5. Solution options

Ranked from cheapest to most radical.

### A. Coordinate anyway (coordinator + matchmaking)
- **What:** one owner, regular cross-university matchmaking, a website and a progress report.
- **Evidence:** Boudreau et al. (2017). Collaboration rises even among similar researchers.
- **Who decides:** Riga City Council plus the universities. **Cost:** low. **Time:** 2027.
- **Limit:** it doesn't remove competition for students.

### B. Student-side consortium (Knowledge Mile Study Agreement)
- **What:** a shared elective catalogue, automatic recognition, up to 2 courses a semester at a partner, reserved seats and home-pays clearing.
- **Evidence:** Colleges of the Fenway (300–400 cross-registrations a semester) and Leiden–Delft–Erasmus (a third of minor places reserved for partners; 7,000+ applications in one round).
- **Why it fits overlap:** students gain from variety *within* similar fields, for example a law student taking RSU's medical law or a business student taking RISEBA's design courses.
- **Who decides:** the five rectors. No law change is needed. **Cost:** low. **Time:** pilot in autumn 2027.

### C. Shared services and infrastructure
- **What:** a joint assessment before any big investment, shared sports facilities, libraries, labs, student housing and a student card.
- **Evidence:** Longwood/MASCO was created explicitly to deal with duplication of services. LaSER's sports-facility example shows the cost of not doing it.
- **Who decides:** the coordinator, universities and city. **Cost:** saves money. **Time:** 2027–2028.

### D. Joint missions built on differences
- **What:** 2–3 cross-university centres or programmes where each partner brings something the others lack. Candidates:
  - **Health and medtech:** RSU (clinical) + RTU (engineering) + LU (sciences);
  - **Health management and economics:** RSU + RISEBA + LU;
  - **Creative technology and urban design:** RISEBA (architecture, media) + RTU (architecture, IT);
  - **Visitor economy and the city:** Turība (tourism) + the city.
- **Evidence:** Montreal QI (ÉTS + McGill), Pittsburgh, Aalto's logic of combining technology, business and art, and the joint minors taught by staff from all three LDE universities.
- **Who decides:** universities plus funders (EU funds, LIAA, the city). **Cost:** medium. **Time:** 2028–2029.

### E. Joint schools for the duplicated fields
- **What:** instead of five small business or law programmes competing, create a joint graduate school or joint master's programme run by several universities.
- **Evidence:**
  - Amsterdam: UvA and VU run joint units, including a joint dentistry school (ACTA) and the merged university medical centre (Amsterdam UMC, 2018).
  - Latvia already does this: RISEBA and Banku augstskola ran a joint doctoral programme in business management.
- **Who decides:** the universities involved, with accreditation by AIC. **Cost:** medium. **Time:** 2029+.

### F. Profiling agreement (division of labour)
- **What:** universities agree, with the ministry, who leads in which field, and state funding rewards it.
- **Evidence:** Dutch sector plans and Finland's PROFI. Latvia already has tools: the 2021 typology law (scientific universities, applied sciences universities, and so on) and performance-based funding following the World Bank's 2014 three-pillar model.
- **Who decides:** the Ministry of Education and Science, the universities and their councils. **Cost:** needs earmarked money. **Time:** 2030+.
- **Limit:** RISEBA and Turība are private, so they can only be invited, not directed.

### G. Merger
- **What:** combining institutions, as already done with Banku augstskola into LU and Liepāja into RTU.
- **Pros:** removes duplication. **Cons:**
  - slow and politically costly;
  - the merger literature warns of "cumbersome" management;
  - impossible for private universities;
  - it would reduce five actors to fewer, which isn't a "Knowledge Mile" policy but a higher-education reform.

## 7. How this changes our argument

- **Old framing:** "five complementary universities just need connecting". This is weak, because they overlap.
- **New framing:**
  1. **Scale:** together the five make Riga a student city of 30,000–40,000 students. Rankings such as QS Best Student Cities judge the *city*, so similar universities still add up.
  2. **Competition:** overlap makes them rivals. That is exactly why they won't cooperate on their own, and why a neutral coordinator is needed.
  3. **Differences:** build the Mile's missions around what is genuinely different (medicine × engineering × design × business).
  4. **Profiling:** let the evidence from steps 1–3 feed a later profiling agreement with the ministry.

**Answer to "if they're the same, there's no Knowledge Mile":**
> "If they were identical, it would be a merger question, not a Knowledge Mile question. But they overlap in what they teach and differ in what they research. The Mile should connect the differences and share the overlap."

## 8. Risks and counterarguments

- **"Cross-registration helps rivals poach students."** Use home-pays clearing and a cap of 2 courses, as in Boston and LDE, so the home university keeps the student and the fee.
- **"Joint missions are just more memoranda."** Make each mission have a budget, a lead institution and KPIs. That is the gap LaSER found in the 2024 memorandum.
- **"The ministry will push mergers anyway."** Coordination is the cheaper, reversible first step. If it fails, that becomes evidence for structural reform.
- **"Our overlap table is incomplete."** It is. Verify it with the AIC register before presenting numbers.
