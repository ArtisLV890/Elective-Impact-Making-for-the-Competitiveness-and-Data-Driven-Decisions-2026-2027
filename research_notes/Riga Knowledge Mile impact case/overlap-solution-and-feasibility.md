# Overlapping universities: our solution and its feasibility

*Riga Knowledge Mile impact case, 9 November 2026*

## The problem

The five Knowledge Mile universities (LU, RTU, RSU, RISEBA and BA Turība) overlap heavily in what they teach:
- business and management is taught at all five;
- law at three;
- communication at four;
- medicine at two.

The overlap is growing. RTU is expanding its Riga Business School, and LU absorbed Banku augstskola in 2025.

Because funding follows students, overlapping universities are **competitors** for the same students and state-funded places. Theory predicts this blocks cooperation. Partners learn most from those who are related but different (Boschma, 2005; Frenken et al., 2007), and competition erodes trust.

LaSER (2026) makes a related point about infrastructure. It warns of a "risk of duplication and unused synergies" (p. 47), giving the example of the LU and RSU sports facilities, and calls for avoiding "excessive mutual competition" (p. 34). The **programme overlap is our own finding**, which goes beyond LaSER.

## Our solution: work around the overlap and use it

We do not propose to make the universities complementary by force. That is a national funding question, and it would take years. Instead, the Knowledge Mile should do three things that the overlap does not block.

**1. A neutral owner.**
Because the universities are rivals, none of them will lead. Riga City Council should appoint a Knowledge Mile coordinator. Its first task is to review all planned investments on the Mile and flag overlaps *before* money is spent, starting with sports facilities, student housing and study spaces.

**2. Use the overlap for students: a Knowledge Mile Study Agreement.**
Similar fields make cross-enrolment easy. For example, a Turība business student could take a RISEBA design course or an RSU health-management course. The agreement would include:
- a shared elective catalogue;
- recognition agreed in advance;
- a cap of 2 courses per semester;
- reserved seats for partner students;
- a **home-pays** rule, so the home university keeps the student and the fee.

Similar consortia exist abroad: Boston's Colleges of the Fenway (300–400 cross-registrations a semester) and Leiden–Delft–Erasmus (a third of minor places reserved for partner students).

**3. Two or three joint missions built on genuine differences.**
For example:
- health and medtech (RSU + RTU + LU);
- creative technology and architecture (RISEBA + RTU).

Each mission has a lead institution, a budget and KPIs. This is where innovation comes from, as in Montreal (ÉTS + McGill) and Pittsburgh (Pitt + CMU). It is also the universities' first real step towards specialisation.

**Long term:** a profiling agreement, in which universities agree who leads in which field and state funding rewards it, as in the Dutch sector plans and Finland's PROFI programme. This remains the coordinator's ask to the Ministry of Education and Science. It is not part of our immediate proposal.

**Not recommended:** a merger. It is impossible for the two private universities, slow and costly, and it would turn a Knowledge Mile policy into a higher-education reform.

## Feasibility

| Element | Feasibility | Why | Main risk and mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coordinator | **High** | Low cost for the city (about €0.6m a year); the Riga 2050 strategy is being drafted now; LaSER recommends it | Political cycles stalled the 2024 memorandum. *Mitigation:* a dedicated post and an annual public progress report |
| Investment review | **High** | Costs nothing; the city owns much of the land and controls transport, which gives it leverage | Universities may see it as interference. *Mitigation:* advisory, not a veto |
| Study Agreement | **Medium–high** | No law forbids it; LU's rules already allow it; tested abroad | Universities cite accreditation rules (LaSER p. 37) and fear losing students. *Mitigation:* home-pays, the 2-course cap and a clause in the regulation replacing Cabinet Regulation No. 793 |
| Joint missions | **Medium** | Needs funding; the timing fits the 2028–2034 EU funding period | Could become "another memorandum". *Mitigation:* each mission needs a budget, a lead and KPIs |
| Profiling agreement | **Low (for now)** | Needs ministry money; RISEBA and Turība are private and can only be invited | Slow and political, so we leave it to the long term |
| Merger | **Not feasible / not recommended** | Impossible for private universities; costly | — |

## Verdict

**The solution is feasible if it is presented as a modest, step-by-step one.** It will not make the five universities complementary. But it will:
- stop new duplication;
- let students use all five universities;
- start real collaboration where the universities genuinely differ.

The weakest point is the universities' incentives: funding follows students, so anything that looks like losing students will be resisted. The home-pays rule and the 2-course cap are designed to make cooperation cost each university nothing.

> *"We can't make five similar universities different overnight. That's a national funding question. But we can stop them duplicating, let students use all five, and connect the few areas where they really differ. That's cheap, legal and can start in 2027."*
